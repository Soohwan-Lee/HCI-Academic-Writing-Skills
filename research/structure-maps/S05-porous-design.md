Source: [P129 Porous by Design](../analyses/P129-porous-design.md), DIS 2024,
author PDF. Structural re-reading 2026-10-31: heading hierarchy and complete
§§5.1–5.2. Rendered discussion pages were previously inspected. Heading descriptions
are normalized. This case shares a venue with S02, but its empirical and conceptual
architecture differs substantially.

The methods include a separate explanation of focus on Care.com. It reports an
analytical focus emerging from the participants' common experience, not a recruitment
criterion to invent retrospectively. Findings retain participant accounts and
interpretation. Discussion relates them to antecedent concepts and worker-centered
commitments; it does not have to culminate in a built solution.

## 2. Complete local paragraph map: §5.1, p.11

1. Reintroduce porosity, recall personal/professional boundary examples, and connect
   asymmetric metrics to power and personhood. This makes the concept's empirical
   referents explicit before claiming an extension.
2. Credit the nearest conceptual account, distinguish the new boundary involving
   perceived care/love and ranking, and illustrate how worker ideals are co-produced
   by clients and platform language. This is lineage → difference → example.
3. Relate uncertainty to invisibility concerns, articulate multiple porous boundaries,
   and propose reciprocal information/screening arrangements. The paragraph moves
   from interpretation to a normative response; prospective design language matters.

Across paragraphs the question changes from “what relation did we see?” to “how
does this change an existing account?” to “what might that imply for responsibility?”
A sequence of generic design guidelines would lose the middle conceptual work.

## 3. Complete local paragraph map: §5.2, pp.11–12

1. Explain worker-perceived advantages of forums: visibility/control and less
   constrained conversation. Describe features and interaction conditions before
   drawing a worker-centered orientation.
2. Complicate the favorable account: unknown ranking, differences between posts
   and profiles, unequal effects of transparency/cooperation, and moderation labor.
   This changes the proposed design criterion rather than simply adding a disclaimer.
3. Motivate wider inquiry, identify relational conditions and an alternative service
   arrangement, then propose how such arrangements might support solidarity and
   understanding. The example is an interpretive resource, not this study's tested
   intervention.

This is benefit → complication → qualified direction. The second paragraph is
necessary to understand what would count as a responsible direction in the third.
Moving it into a generic final Limitations section would weaken that dependency.

## 4. Sentence-level maps

**§5.1 second paragraph, p.11, six sentences:** credit prior boundary account →
explain its visibility relation → distinguish workers' personhood/care boundary →
explain an ideal co-produced by clients and platform → give a job-ad example →
interpret the relation between idealization and identity. The subject chain passes
from prior scholars to workers to clients/platforms and back to the analytical
claim; each change corresponds to a different role in the argument.

**§5.2 second paragraph, p.11, eight sentences:** introduce unresolved trade-offs →
identify perceived symmetry and missing ranking knowledge → distinguish the ranked
objects → reject a universal remedy using prior work → name unequal transparency
consequences → introduce a cooperative counterexample with uneven inclusion →
extend the uneven-benefits concern → identify the distribution of moderation labor.
The paragraph develops one question—whose benefit and whose burden—through several
forms of evidence. Sentence count alone is no reason to split away the qualification
that earns the final responsibility claim.

## 5. Conditional transfer

For a situated social account, organize findings around meaningful relations or
practices, with nested contrasts that develop the interpretation. Interview questions
and participant demographics need not become the findings headings. In discussion,
show where the account reinforces, differentiates or extends a lens before proposing
changes in practice.

Compared with S03, participant material here is not primarily a requirements source.
Compared with S02, the main material is worker accounts interpreted through a care
lens rather than a retrospective portfolio of making. Compared with S04, a theme
and a theoretical extension are not factorial comparisons with effect estimates.
Each can support substantive knowledge; none needs to imitate the others' endpoint.
