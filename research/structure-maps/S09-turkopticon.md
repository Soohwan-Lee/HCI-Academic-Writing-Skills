# S09 — Activist infrastructure, maintenance and ambivalent success

Source: [P112 Turkopticon](../analyses/P112-turkopticon.md), CHI 2013,
proceedings-format author copy. Re-read local system/discussion sequences on PDF
pp.6–8 (printed pp.616–618), 2026-10-31. Prior full reading and visual inspection
are recorded in P112. This is a participatory/activist infrastructure contrast, not
a universal co-design protocol or evidence of community ownership in every respect.

## 1. Actual hierarchy

Introduction → Method and Our Stance → AMT background → motivation → system →
discussion → ambivalence of activist success → unheaded recap.

- Background splits the service abstraction from the work of employing people.
- Motivation moves through worker rights, fairness and mutual aid/accountability.
- System moves through reputation standardization, collective-system bootstrapping,
  retaliation/reputation tensions and moderation.
- Discussion moves through maintenance/repair, tactical quantification and publics.
- The final substantive section reconsiders the meaning and cost of success.

Method/stance precedes the background and system story, orienting how the authors'
involvement produced the account. Literature is distributed where it historicizes,
explains infrastructure, justifies choices or reframes compromise. There is no
standalone controlled Evaluation section. Adoption and years of maintenance are
not interchangeable with improved wages or causal protection from retaliation.

System subsections are organized around socio-organizational problems, not just
frontend/backend architecture. Discussion returns to the same material at another
analytical level: a moderation feature becomes maintenance work; ratings become a
political compromise; feedback becomes a means of association.

## 2. Complete paragraph map: bootstrapping, printed pp.616–617

1. Establish the critical-mass problem and why separated/invisible workers make
   community formation difficult.
2. Explain the partnership that supplied paid initial reviews and how it let early
   users benefit before contributing.
3. Interpret the alliance through divergent partner commitments and partial
   connection, rather than presenting recruitment as a politically neutral success.

The sequence is infrastructure dependency → practical arrangement → political
interpretation. A conventional implementation-only rewrite would preserve the
seed-data step but erase why the alliance matters to the contribution.

## 3. Complete paragraph map: maintenance/repair, printed p.618

1. Establish continuing technical maintenance and give changing-browser/server examples.
2. Add changing requirements as practices and norms shift, returning to moderation
   and an interface change.
3. Explain the relationships through which maintainers learn and act, positioning
   moderators as knowledgeable participants rather than merely research subjects.
4. Interpret maintenance as more than requirements collection and connect it to a
   disciplinary argument about collective political capacity.

The paragraphs broaden from code upkeep to changing practice, then relationships,
then political interpretation. They are not four examples of a single maintenance
category. The last paragraph's solidarity claim needs to retain its situated
interpretive status; this architecture does not substitute for evidence.

## 4. Sentence-level maps

**Bootstrapping final paragraph, printed p.617, four sentences:** name an ambivalent
alliance → explain partner's market-repair reading → contrast the authors' political
aim → connect the coalition to partial-connection theory. Stable actor distinctions
make the contrast readable. The source's double-edged metaphor is stronger than
its explicitly stated cost; an adapted draft should name the actual tension.

**Maintenance final paragraph, printed p.618, five sentences:** move beyond
requirements → interpret solidarity → cite a critique of individualizing market
frames → extend that concern to network frames → propose a different disciplinary
orientation. This climbs levels of analysis; it does not report five new empirical
findings. The final call follows from the position and situated account.

## 5. Conditional transfer

For participatory/infrastructural work, organize the account around relationships,
negotiations and sustaining arrangements when those are the knowledge contribution.
A useful system description can explain who can contribute, decide, contest and
maintain, and why those arrangements were selected. A later discussion can reinterpret
these same arrangements without repeating their technical operation.

Retain the authors' role and concrete participation channels. Do not replace them
with generic co-design claims. A final ambivalence section can examine how useful
intervention also reproduces an infrastructure's constraints. It is different from
a generic small-sample limitation and different from a declaration that building
has solved the political problem. Compare S01's participatory embodiment refinement:
participation can play a narrower role there without invalidating either structure.
