# P72 — Portfolio annotations that preserve temporal and relational knowledge

## Source and scope

Culén, Børsting, and Gaver, *Strategies for Annotating Portfolios: Mapping Designs for New Domains*, DIS 2020, DOI 10.1145/3357236.3395490. Read the complete main argument and inspected the annotated figures in the [Goldsmiths author accepted manuscript](https://research.gold.ac.uk/29054/7/Annotation%20Strategies%20AAM.pdf). The first page explicitly labels this version as accepted for DIS 2020. Award unverified. Single-analyst rhetorical coding selects the paper for a concrete alternative to treating abstraction as the sole way of communicating design knowledge. Its historical health descriptions and speculative device combinations are analyzed as manuscript claims, not adopted as medical guidance or validated treatment effects.

## Architecture and rhetorical moves

Introduction → Intermediate Knowledge in Design Research → The Portfolio (AV1, RelaxMe, SlowBreath, ShareME) → Annotation Strategies → Discussion (collective viewing, trajectory, ecosystem) → Conclusion. The portfolio is introduced before its comparative annotations so readers can understand what the annotations refer to. The discussion revisits the strategies' knowledge implications rather than introducing an unrelated generic implications list.

The abstract states an extension to annotated portfolios, acknowledges their intended openness, identifies how existing use tends toward abstraction, proposes trajectory and ecosystem strategies, then names the four-design domain application. Introduction pp. 1–2 begins with designerly knowledge debates, differentiates existing intermediate forms, and motivates a domain where situated design relationships matter. It acknowledges earlier technology supporting people with ME before specifying how this work begins from design practice and iteratively develops a portfolio. The gap is a restricted use of an existing knowledge form; it does not claim that the domain contains no technology or research.

The related-work section compares design tools, guidelines, patterns, experiential qualities, strong concepts, and portfolios according to the kinds of knowledge they communicate. It argues that moving toward general theory may lose situated qualities. This prepares the proposed contribution: chronology and complementarity can reveal useful knowledge without substantial abstraction. The literature therefore establishes an evaluative problem and a conceptual alternative, not a list of fashionable design methods.

## Support, warrant, and limits

The primary knowledge object is a methodological extension demonstrated through a design portfolio. Research-through-design, prior deployments, interviews, workshops, first-person exploration, and annotation are means of producing and reflecting on that knowledge. The four artifacts are not four equivalent experiments: AV1 was observed with nine adolescents over approximately a year; RelaxMe and SlowBreath had smaller, shorter engagements; ShareME used Wizard-of-Oz enactment rather than a fully implemented system. Those differences matter when reading benefit language.

The trajectory strategy connects successive design motivations; the ecosystem strategy examines possible relationships that need not follow development order. Figures and accompanying narratives provide the main comparative warrant. The authors explicitly state that all four were not deployed together. An ecosystem scenario consequently demonstrates a way to reason about complementarity, not evidence that the combined devices produce its imagined outcomes. Claims about wider communication and responsible future design are proposed benefits of the annotation approach. A derivative writing skill should preserve this distinction even where the paper's wording becomes confident.

## Two rendered paragraph analyses

PDF p. 7, Trajectory strategy, opening **“What is interesting about”**, has two sentences. S1 interprets the preceding historical account as jointly representing time and design concerns. S2 derives its generative implication: exposing further possibilities within or beyond the current space. The preceding paragraph narrates transitions among the artifacts; the following offers speculative new-device examples. This short paragraph performs an interpretive bridge between evidence and possibilities. It neither needs padding to meet a paragraph quota nor supplies independent evaluation evidence.

PDF p. 10, Discussion/Gains from ecosystem strategy, opening **“Although we did not have”**, has three sentences. S1 acknowledges the missing combined deployment while advocating ecosystem reasoning. S2 narrows the intended contribution to reflective tools that might inform planning. S3 proposes succinct communication of relationships and motivations. The preceding paragraph discusses complementary and alternative device relations; the following considers communication beyond academia. Its opening mixes limitation and claim, so identifying it merely as a deductive topic sentence would miss the qualification. The later modal language is essential to the scope of the inference.

## Transfer conditions and template exceptions

When a portfolio yields relational or historical knowledge, show the artifacts and the relation that makes comparison productive. Different annotation strategies may answer different questions over the same material. Report implementation and deployment differences before readers encounter synthetic claims. Chronological order is justified only when development dependencies are evidenced; an ecosystem interpretation cannot establish chronology. This contribution need not end in a generalized theory or a uniformly evaluated artifact suite to be intelligible and assessable.
