## What the contribution is

The knowledge object is a situated retrospective autoethnography through design, including a singular set of physicalisations and an account of how making and handling them supports reflection. Sensor data are design material, not a quantitative experiment. The micro-phenomenological interview and team discussion are reflective inquiry among the authors, not independent validation by an external user sample. The work combines eight months of dance learning and data collection with approximately a year of analysis, modelling, and fabrication. Fourteen rehearsals yielded recordings of three choreographies; the final series uses selected recordings and one wrist-accelerometer variable from Dubinushka.

Actual sequence: Introduction; Origin of the Project (team, repertoire, motivation); Related Work; Methods; Learning the Duncan Dance Repertoire and Collecting Data; Designing the Artefacts; Interacting with the Data (methods and findings); Discussion; Conclusion; acknowledgments; references. An explicit shift to first-person singular in §§5–6 marks whose experience and agency the narrative describes. The second methods section supplies the later reflective inquiry's procedure rather than duplicating initial data collection.

The abstract states approach, materials and transformation, then explains methodological value and how the representations mediate understanding. Introduction pp 1–2 starts with encounters with personal data and physicalisation, narrows to this embodied-learning project, narrates its collection-to-making-to-reflection trajectory, and ends with three contributions. A separate origin section makes the collaborators' expertise and Duncan transmission legible. The research question is embedded in motivation and practice rather than imposed as experimental hypotheses.

## Literature and evidence

Related work §§3.1–3.3 connects first-person methods, bodily/dance knowledge, and physicalising data. Each strand establishes a different warrant: intimate experience can be research material; universal notation can miss situated dance knowledge; physicalisations may support reflection beyond productivity. Positioning therefore concerns a way of knowing and documenting, not merely an absent interface. Existing work is often introduced through concrete examples, followed by a paragraph stating this project's relationship to that tradition.

The argument relies on recorded movement, journals, remembered experience, documented design iterations, images, and researcher discussion. Failed clay prints and illegible mappings explain later simplification and casting choices. Figures constitute evidence of the representational decisions; removing them while preserving only prose would flatten the argument. Intended intensity mappings are distinguished from an emergent grounding interpretation. The latter is meaningful within the reflective practice but is not a validated physical measurement of grounding.

Discussion moves from reflexive process to emergent meaning, aesthetic ambiguity, and implications beyond the researcher's self. It confronts the researcher-centering problem directly. Transfer concerns methodological possibilities and documentation practices; it does not establish that other dancers would interpret the artifacts similarly. ## Two micro-analyses

**p 9, §6.2.2, “Overall, we realised during” (four sentence units, including final fragment).** S 1 states the derived simplification lesson; S 2 qualifies it by recalling prior dataset reduction; S 3 announces two resulting commitments and specifies one recording per object; S 4 adds one data mapping per physical property. The preceding paragraph reports lost interpretability when multiple properties were encoded. The next subsection explains fabrication and material constraints. This is conclusion-first local design reasoning: a decision follows documented difficulty, not an unexplained stylistic preference for minimalism.

**p 14, §8.2, “Eventually, the curves in” (six sentences).** S 1 identifies intentionally encoded information; S 2 locates its visible manifestation; S 3 contrasts the unintended grounding-related base; S 4 clarifies that this meaning was absent in earlier sensor analysis; S 5 relates the phenomenon to a literature concept; S 6 records when the interpretation emerged. The preceding one-sentence paragraph introduces surprise; the next describes the dance expert's initial skepticism and later interpretation. The opening is retrospective setup, not a general conclusion. This paragraph makes agency, temporal order, and interpretive provenance inspectable.

**Transfer.** Write material transformations as accountable reasoning and distinguish designed mappings from emergent readings. Preserve subjective voice and ambiguity when they are part of the knowledge claim; do not recast this work as a sensor-accuracy study or require engineering-style design implications.
