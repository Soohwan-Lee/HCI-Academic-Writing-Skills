## Methodological proposal and its limits

This paper proposes a model for interaction-design research and criteria for assessing contributions made through that model. It is not simply an artifact paper that uses RtD. Its empirical inputs include a workshop, interviews, and iterative discussions, alongside literature and illustrative design cases. Those inputs inform the model; they do not establish its superiority through a comparative intervention study.

The evaluation-criteria section (printed pp. 499–500) offers four lenses: process, invention, relevance, and extensibility. In this model, readers need enough account of methods and choices to understand the inquiry; a situated account of what the artifact integrates; an explanation of the present situation and the preferred alternative; and documentation that lets others build on the knowledge. These are attributable methodological proposals. They should not be presented as criteria universally accepted by all RtD traditions.

The relevance lens asks authors to justify the desirable state their work pursues. A writing skill can therefore request an explicit account of whose situation is being changed, what is valued, and why the proposed direction is defensible. Asking whose values are represented is an editorial extension, not a quotation from this source. Similarly, extensibility motivates explaining transferable insights and their conditions without claiming statistical generalization.

The paper emphasizes that repeating a design process need not reproduce an identical artifact. This is not an exemption from documenting decisions or supporting claims. Its broad disciplinary contrasts, including a narrow characterization of validity in behavioral research, are historical framing rather than a complete account of contemporary methodological standards. Do not encode validity as synonymous with null-hypothesis rejection in the skill.

**Applicability:** when making and reflecting are the means of generating knowledge. A prototype built only to deliver an experimental treatment may belong primarily to an empirical route. A new engineering capability may need an artifact route. Hybrids must state which design insights, technical capabilities, or observations are contributed, and which activities provide evidence.

## Observed writing architecture

Top-level sections proceed through Introduction (including term definitions) → Methodology → Literature Review → Interviews → proposed model → evaluation criteria → illustrative design cases → Conclusion. Long section labels are paraphrased here. The Literature Review follows the methodological overview because the paper reports an inquiry into how design research might participate in HCI. It should not be automatically moved ahead of Methods by a template-driven editor.

The abstract moves from a disciplinary integration problem to a proposed model, then explains how that model creates knowledge and previews criteria and cases. The introduction begins from the authors' participation in an institutional problem, identifies lack of an agreed model, and states the inquiry's aims. Subsequent paragraphs diagnose the lost opportunity and name two contributions. It then defines contested terms before giving a roadmap. The contribution is qualified as an additional model rather than the only legitimate approach.

Prior work establishes intellectual ancestry and differences: accounts of design process, critical design, design practice, and artifact-centered knowledge enable the proposal's positioning. Novelty is integration and a particular role for designers, not a claim that design or RtD began with this article. The later examples illustrate how the proposed lenses operate; they are not a representative test set demonstrating universal validity.

## Paragraph and sentence flow

**Locator:** Introduction, printed p. 493, opening “In recent years”. Three sentences, visually checked: **S1** situates the authors as witnesses and participants in efforts to combine design, technology, and behavioral science (grounded context); **S2** contrasts enthusiasm with a missing agreed research model (tension and gap); **S3** describes a sustained inquiry and its two aims (response and scope). The opening establishes a situation; the central gap arrives in S2. This is not a conclusion-first paragraph.

There is no earlier introduction paragraph. The next paragraph states what the inquiry exposed and why the lack of a shared vision matters; the following paragraph names the contributions. The progression is situated tension → interpreted consequence → proposed response. A writer can use that logic without copying its autobiographical voice or treating personal participation alone as sufficient evidence.

## Adaptation for an ACM manuscript

When editing an RtD `.tex` draft, connect each important design decision to its motivation, alternatives or tensions, artifact manifestation, reflection, and resulting knowledge claim. Retain the design trajectory where it carries reasoning, rather than converting the manuscript mechanically into an experiment report. Make figures and artifact descriptions do argumentative work: point to what they reveal, not merely what was built.

Separate the proposal's intended effects from effects actually observed in use. If the source material lacks a reflection, rationale, or transfer condition, flag that omission rather than manufacturing a polished account. The relevant unit is a supported design insight, not a compulsory numbered RQ or a fixed number of iterations. This editing workflow is an editorial adaptation of the methodological proposal, not a measured writing norm.
