## Contribution contract

The primary contribution is a critical/normative argument and a practical set of
questions for judging when a computational intervention may be inappropriate.
Its warrant is conceptual reasoning with published illustrative cases. It does not
report a new comparative study demonstrating that the proposed questions improve
outcomes, nor does it systematically sample all sustainable-HCI projects. The
conclusion does not reject technology in general: it asks the field to recognize
and articulate limits, alternatives and situations in which building is justified.

## Architecture, opening and positioning

Actual sequence: Abstract; An Explosion; Articulating the Value of Absence
(low/no-tech, more harm than good, computational transformations); Discussion
(from solutions to problems, beyond problems); Arguments for Practice;
Acknowledgments; References. There is no separate methods or related-work section.
The form follows its argument rather than pretending to be an empirical report.

The opening starts with HCI's expanding domains, identifies insufficient attention
to inappropriate interventions, takes a position, previews three evaluative
questions and situates sustainability as the central illustration. The abstract
likewise moves from expanded application to the need for critical judgment, then
to questions, literature illustrations and practical consequences. A writing agent
should preserve this argument-based contribution rather than invent RQs, subjects
or experimental findings to fill a conventional template.

Related scholarship performs work throughout the paper. Prior cases make the three
questions concrete; discussion identifies adjacent ideas and distinguishes the
paper's claim from merely omitting features; the final position connects the
argument with broader approaches to situated technology. This is not a claim that
conceptual papers never need an explicit related-work section.

## Evidence and analytical movement

The three questions progressively shift attention from alternative solutions to
what problem the technology represents. Gardening illustrates low-tech expertise
and technological maintenance burdens. Producer–consumer connections expose a
larger economic arrangement. Worship illustrates an intervention potentially
undermining the experience it supports. Carbon accounting illustrates excluded or
incommensurable effects. These cases substantiate questions and reasoning, not
measured benefits of the proposed non-technological alternatives.

Discussion then questions problem–solution framing itself, proposing attention to
interventions within complex situations. The practical ending makes refusal,
abandoned alternatives, removal of technology and explanations of failure legible
as research possibilities. It distinguishes an analytically useful failure account
from simply reporting that people disliked a system. Even a favorable evaluation
does not automatically justify widespread adoption. The stance is consequential
without being an effectiveness claim.

## Two complete paragraph micro-analyses

**PDF p.3, From Solutions to Problems, sole paragraph (three sentences).** S1 defines
solution-space exploration in familiar design terms. S2 supplies the complementary
problem-space operation using parallel syntax. S3 maps the preceding three questions
onto the progression between them. The paragraph turns a list of practical questions
into a structured intellectual argument. The prior discussion locates neighboring
scholarship; the next subsection challenges the shared problem–solution premise.
Topic-first writing here introduces an accessible distinction before making the
larger claim, rather than leading with the most abstract conclusion.

**PDF p.3, Discussion, opening paragraph (four sentences).** S1 limits the target of
critique; S2 identifies the field-level object; S3 states how the examples function;
S4 positions the argument alongside related approaches. The repeated referent is
the paper's argumentative act, while each sentence clarifies a different boundary.
The preceding carbon-accounting example ends by broadening to systemic issues;
the next paragraph specifies both affinities and differences with existing work.
This is useful where critical examples could otherwise be mistaken for attacks on
individual projects. It is not a stock disclaimer to add to every discussion.

## Transfer and counterexample

For a non-design argument, make the evaluative premise, concrete situations,
reasoning and practical consequence inspectable. State who can judge appropriateness
and what remains uncertain when adapting the pattern. Do not turn illustrative
alternatives into demonstrated solutions, or this paper's stance into a blanket
rule against building. Compare P48's concern with implications-for-design and P49's
normative agenda: similar broad routes still have different argumentative jobs.
