# P76 — Reflective Informatics

## Source and contribution

Eric P. S. Baumer, *Reflective Informatics: Conceptual Dimensions for Designing Technologies of Reflection*, CHI 2015, DOI 10.1145/2702123.2702234. The Cornell-hosted proceedings-format PDF has ten pages, printed 585–594, with Cornell affiliation. Publisher metadata verifies identity. Award status is unverified. All main text was read; two paragraphs were visually inspected in rendered pages. This is single-analyst, version-dependent rhetorical coding.

The knowledge object is conceptual grounding and three generative dimensions: breakdown, inquiry, and transformation. The method is selective interdisciplinary theoretical synthesis followed by interpretation of existing designs. There is no new participant study, intervention trial, or systematic retrieval protocol. The paper explicitly excludes an exhaustive theoretical review. Calling it a conceptual synthesis preserves the distinction between how the argument is built and what knowledge it contributes.

## Organization and opening argument

Actual sections are Introduction; Related Work, including Personal Informatics, Reflective Design, and Slow Technology; Theoretical Background, organized as Philosophical, Cognitive, Educational, Critical, and Summary Notes; Some Dimensions of Reflective Informatics, with the three dimensions and examples; Discussion and Future; Conclusion; acknowledgments and references. The discussion includes reflection's assumed value, power/privilege, and evaluation. It applies the paper's own concepts reflexively instead of adding only generic future work.

The abstract opens with interest coupled to conceptual under-specification, states review/engagement as the approach, names three outputs, explains their intended utility and relation to existing work, and ends with critical self-examination. The introduction uses a philosophical epigraph, then identifies definitional fuzziness and disciplinary fragmentation. It recognizes that designing for reflection already exists. It previews synthesis, cautions against turning the new term into a disciplinary silo, and states two contributions. This provides novelty through conceptual clarification and connection, without inventing a first-ever topic claim or empirical RQ.

Related Work distinguishes designers' reflection from users' reflection, then examines three relevant traditions. Each discussion gives the prior approach credit and specifies what the proposed dimensions add. Theoretical Background does different work: it supplies the intellectual materials for synthesis. Its Summary Notes compare individual versus socially situated reflection and different value judgments about reflective thought. Thus, source-by-source exposition becomes warranted synthesis through an explicit cross-perspective comparison. A writer should preserve this intermediate argumentative step rather than jumping from famous theorists to three unsupported labels.

## Claim, support, warrant, and limits

The main claim is that shared dimensions can clarify reflection and inform design. Support comes from conceptual resonances across theories and worked readings of systems. The warrant is explanatory and generative usefulness: the dimensions make meaningful distinctions, suggest design possibilities, and expose tensions in existing examples. The dimensions are neither independent variables nor sequential stages, and the paper presents them as revisable, non-prescriptive tools.

Prior systems illustrate interpretive fit; they do not independently validate all three dimensions. ChatterBox can be read as requiring a transformed perspective or as constraining users to one perspective, demonstrating that a concept supports contestable interpretation. The discussion questions whether reflection is always desirable and who has the opportunity to reflect. Evaluation proposals distinguish kinds of reflection from quantities and warn against treating a desirable outcome as proof that reflection occurred. These are methodological positions argued here, not universal prohibitions on quantitative research.

## Paragraph micro-analysis B

Printed p.592, Transformation/Examples, locator “Similarly, the lens of”: three sentences, complete boundaries visually checked. S1 announces interpretive multiplicity. S2 develops a favorable reading: making sense of ChatterBox requires transformation. S3 develops the counter-reading: requiring one stance may fail to support movement through transformation. The previous paragraph links breakdown and transformation and questions whether slowness is sufficient. The following Discussion and Future turns this critical reflexivity onto the paper itself. This paragraph intentionally ends with an unresolved counterargument; adding a confident summary would erase its function.

## Transferable choices

Use related work for positioning and theoretical background for constructing an argument when those roles genuinely differ. Explain how synthesis handles differences before extracting common concepts. Demonstrate a framework through both illuminating and difficult cases, then articulate its commitments and limits. This structure suits conceptual work; it does not justify replacing evidence of reflection with a system's design intention in an empirical paper. Nor does the repeated dimension-plus-example organization establish a universal section ratio or paragraph length.
