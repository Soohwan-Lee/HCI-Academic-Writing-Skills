## Architecture and argument

Actual sequence: Introduction → Literature Review → Background (Apollo Mission; Core Memory Technology) → Our Project (Methodology; Design process; Data collection; Data analysis; The Core Memory Quilt) → Making Core Memory (building, preparation, workshops, and metaphors of production) → Discussion (System Behaviors versus Engaging Systems; Building Sympathetic Contexts; Materializing Absences) → Conclusion. The design and empirical narrative is organized around meaningful episodes, not a separate usability-evaluation section.

The abstract names the hidden-work inquiry, theoretical orientation, quilt and workshops, interpretive contribution, and methodological reach. It does not promise a superior interactive textile. The introduction begins with a claim about methods shaping who counts as a designer, moves to gendered histories, focuses on memory weaving, explains the project’s two phases, and differentiates three contributions. Those contributions concern historical expertise, intertwined manual/cognitive labor, and historical-participatory inquiry. The concrete object arrives as a means of investigating these claims rather than as the inevitable solution to a user problem.

Literature Review joins craft, feminist technology histories, fabrication, cultural heritage, and electronic textiles. It argues that craft histories change understandings of technical expertise and design methods. Background then supplies technical-historical details needed to understand the intervention. Keeping these separate avoids forcing every historical explanation to perform a novelty claim. Prior work is revisited within episodes and discussion as an interpretive resource, not exhausted in an opening list.

## Evidence and its boundaries

Archival resources, correspondence with engineers, design-team fieldnotes, workshop recordings, and material breakdowns support the account. Three deliberately chosen workshops engage technology historians/practitioners, educators/designers, and interested members of the public. Analysis uses reflective memos and iterative thematic interpretation. The primary warrant is triangulation between embodied making and historical discourse: difficulties with weaving and repairs expose forms of expertise obscured by descriptions of routine assembly. The quilt’s technical behavior enables these encounters; it is not itself proof that historical workers had particular experiences.

The authors explicitly lack first-person accounts from the original weavers and describe the choice to use available managerial/engineering voices. The conclusion returns to this absence rather than claiming complete recovery. Workshop empathy is not equivalence with historical labor. Similarly, the narrative reframes expertise without romanticizing gendered work as natural love or patience. Discussion earns implications by connecting concrete solder/thread breakdowns and workshop skill differences to the contrast between material capacities and the practices that make them possible.

## Transfer and exception

Use material incidents to connect levels of analysis, but state what the incident can and cannot establish. A critical design narrative can integrate historical context, construction, and participants without calling every encounter evaluation. Do not invent missing historical voices, infer representative educational effects, or sanitize failed construction into seamless implementation. When writing engineering performance claims, this narrative warrant needs complementary technical evidence; when writing historical claims, reenactment cannot replace source criticism.
