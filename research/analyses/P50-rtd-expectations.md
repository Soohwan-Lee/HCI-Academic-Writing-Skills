# P50 — What Should We Expect From Research Through Design?

**Identity.** William Gaver, CHI 2012, DOI 10.1145/2207676.2208538. Institutional author accepted manuscript: https://research.gold.ac.uk/14818/7/WHAT%20SHOULD%20WE%20EXPECT%20AAM.pdf. The front page explicitly labels this version AAM; PDF-page locators apply. Award unverified. Primary contribution: conceptual/meta-theoretical argument about research through design, not an empirical evaluation of a new artifact. Single-analyst coding.

## Architecture and opening

Actual main sequence: Introduction → Theory in Research Through Design → Scientific and Design Theories → Convergence in Research Through Design → Theory as Annotation → Conclusion. Nested sections introduce falsifiability and scientific research programmes, explain underspecification and generativity, examine three interpretations of non-convergence, and develop annotated portfolios. A large portfolio illustration arrives near the end, where the proposed account needs a concrete referent.

The abstract explicitly calls the work an essay, maps its three parts, gives each part’s argumentative outcome, and closes with a stance toward standardization. Introduction defines the practice and its outputs, quotes calls for consolidation, then acknowledges their appeal before identifying risks of restrictive self-policing and imported scientific assumptions. The final introductory paragraph previews claims and argumentative operations together. It does not promise a hypothesis test, new system, or exhaustive literature review.

There is no separate Related Work. Literature performs several differentiated jobs: map the types of conceptual work already produced; articulate an opposing position accurately; provide contrasting philosophical accounts; supply design examples; and support a constructive alternative. The essay recognizes implicit conceptualization, translated concepts, manifestos, frameworks, and meta-theory rather than treating all theory as a single product. Its novelty lies in connecting expectations about theory, interpretations of disciplinary diversity, and the role of particular artifacts.

## Argument, evidence, and scope

The central position is that design theory is provisional, contingent, and aspirational, and that annotation of realized examples and portfolios deserves recognition. The support is conceptual comparison, engagement with design scholarship, and illustrative artifacts. The warrant depends on design’s synthetic and generative character: theory cannot specify every consequential choice, while an artifact commits to particulars. Comparing Popper and Lakatos also makes the standard of “scientific” inquiry itself contested. The text explicitly says these two accounts do not represent all philosophy of science.

The three accounts of non-convergence are alternatives, not three independently measured causes: immature paradigms, hidden agreement, and the productive plurality of worlds design creates. Their coexistence helps contest an automatic inference from diversity to methodological failure. The Rams portfolio illustrates mutual interpretation between artifacts and annotations; it does not demonstrate experimentally that portfolios improve design outcomes. The argument advocates a way to value knowledge, not universal superiority of one reporting format.

The conclusion retains a boundary that derivative guidance must preserve: annotated portfolios do not subsume every form of theoretical writing. Nor does the paper justify exempting a concrete causal or performance claim from evidence. Its objections concern importing a single account of theory testing into all research through design. Foundational RtD standards should therefore be presented alongside this disagreement, rather than fused into an apparently consensual checklist.

## Paragraph micro-analysis

**PDF p. 8, Theory as Annotation, “If artefacts embody theory” (three sentences; rendered).** S1 qualifies two preceding metaphors: embodied theory is not self-explanatory, and a design-space point does not announce useful dimensions. S2 proposes theory’s explanatory role in making choices and rationales accessible. S3 draws the consequence that theory can annotate particulars rather than subordinating examples to abstraction. Relations: limitation of prior claim → constructive response → conceptual conclusion. The preceding paragraph describes movement through design space; the following subsection extends from one artifact to portfolios. Its first sentence is a qualifying bridge, not the paragraph’s concluding proposition.

## Transfer and exception

An argumentative paper can advance by making alternative explanations explicit, showing what each permits, and developing a constructive proposal after critique. Distinguish philosophical grounds from observed evidence, and indicate when an example illustrates rather than verifies. This essay’s strong interpretive voice and first-person positioning suit its declared genre. A systematic review needs a different coverage warrant; an artifact-performance paper needs an operational account of its evaluation. Neither should inherit this essay’s absence of Methods merely because both belong to HCI.
