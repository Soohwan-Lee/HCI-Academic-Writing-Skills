# P128 — Design Bookkeeping: Making Practice Intelligible through a Managerial Lens

## Source and review scope
Elizabeth Meiklejohn, Laura Devendorf and Irene Posch, DIS 2024 pictorial; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3643834.3660754). Analyzed the [NSF-hosted PDF](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10565271), 15 pages, printed pp.35–49. Root read and visually inspected all 12 main-text pages (printed pp.35–46). Opus 5.5 medium assisted with text-only analysis; root adjudicated its visual hypotheses against the actual pages. The linked external catalog was not examined. [Official DIS awards](https://dis.acm.org/2024/best-papers-at-dis2024/) confirm Best Pictorial. Reviewed 2026-10-30; citation count not collected.

## Contribution, method and form
The pictorial names and makes visible the managerial work supporting a material-led design collaboration. Six collections of entries illustrate a vocabulary of ledgers and bookkeeping, followed by cross-cutting observations about scope, audience and relation to ideation. This is reflective design knowledge with a conceptual and methodological invitation. The e-textile installation supplies the situated material; this paper does not primarily establish an input device's comparative performance.

First-person retrospection examines a three-month collaboration. A weaver-in-residence worked alongside one researcher, with another joining remotely through weekly meetings. Roles, resources and expertise matter to the account. The bookkeeping metaphor emerged through reflection and shaped further retrospective questions. The six cases are not independent replications or a representative population sample. Their purpose is to make particular practices intelligible and potentially adaptable.

## Architecture and opening
Abstract/introduction → completed installation → collaboration and exploratory trajectory → metaphor, method and conceptual resources → six consistently organized visual cases → cross-cutting discussion → possible cultivation and circulation → conclusion. The installation appears before detailed conceptual framing so readers can locate otherwise mundane wire, notation and maintenance work.

The abstract has five sentences: research trajectory, contribution/context, reflective lens, synthesis preview and speculative circulation. It joins an ongoing effort rather than claiming no prior work exists. It previews findings more than it states their substance; an assistant can improve specificity without inventing a measured benefit.

The introduction has three paragraphs. First, several material-oriented research traditions are grouped by their shared commitments and documentation challenges. Second, the challenge narrows to the collaborators' concrete work with loom instructions, brittle materials and machinery. Third, method, six cases, active document roles and community contribution are previewed. No formal hypothesis or labeled RQ is necessary for this argument.

## Related work and theory use
Literature is distributed across the introduction and the conceptual page. Existing portfolios, stories, recipes, zines and tools locate the nearest documentation approaches. A clinical-record analogy supplies a distinction between demonstrating that work occurred and making ongoing work intelligible. The authors explicitly identify their reading as creative, then use it to refine their question.

This is a generative use of prior concepts, not a test of an imported theory. The bookkeeping metaphor itself has an acknowledged limit: knowledge is not simply currency. Its changing roles as lens, genre and practice should remain explicit. A writer should also keep clear whether a ledger means an artifact, project or collection of entries when moving among these scales.

## How the visual cases argue
Each case has a claim-like title, identifies the entries, explains their emergence and interprets what they reveal. PDF pp.5–10 use the same left-hand analytical structure with numbered images and captions on the right. This supports comparison while retaining different materials and layouts.

The first case links a movement sketch to layer notation, a machine draft and cloth. The second juxtaposes a color dictionary, weave structures and prototype to expose dependencies. The third makes hidden wire handling visible through close-up sequences. The fourth compares a craft counting technique with coil winding. The fifth connects revision logs to material changes and a rotated weave direction. The sixth uses arrows to trace adaptations between earlier, current and later projects. These images are evidence and analytical objects, not optional decoration. The overview on p.3 also preserves branches and abandoned directions rather than showing an inevitable path to success.

Reading page images confirmed these roles and the inline numbered references. It did not establish that a novice can reproduce the practices from the pictures. Some text-to-image pointers are imperfect: the p.9 rotation discussion points toward notes while the explicit rotation comparison is in the third image group. Match claims to the relevant visual evidence when adapting this structure.

## Micro-analysis 2 — PDF p.7 / printed p.41, interpretation of wire-management entries
Six sentences: (1) distinguish embodied routing knowledge from aspects visible in pictures; (2) identify their usual invisibility; (3) show how the entries foreground them; (4) bound the solution to a workable local arrangement; (5) supply the concrete observation that connections survived shipping and remote installation; (6) explain usefulness as a reference point despite modest standalone scope. This preserves value while limiting the claim. The shipping observation is practical evidence for that instance, not a durability benchmark or controlled effectiveness test.

## Flow between cases and discussion
Case 2 contrasts maintenance-oriented records with the preceding sense-making notation. Case 3 changes from exploring possibilities to solving necessary implementation problems. Case 4 inherits that necessity but adds deliberate impracticality. Case 5 links unsuccessful paths back to future-self documentation. Case 6 expands from a project to circulation among projects. Discussion then changes analytical scale from entry collections to a documentation genre and from observed practice to possible future cultivation.

These links explain differences; transition words alone would not reproduce the reasoning. Cases can also be entered separately, but references to earlier pages mean their nonlinearity is partial rather than absolute.

## Limits and transfer
The case-selection rationale and retrospective analytical procedure are not described as a formal coding protocol. Do not fabricate one or demand intercoder statistics as a condition of reflective knowledge. First-person reflection is deliberately situated. Recommendations to undertake complex, impractical work should retain the residency's time and budget conditions rather than becoming universal efficiency advice.

The authors acknowledge that specialized entries may remain illegible to much of DIS and cannot replace working together. Transfer can mean adaptation or inspiration among knowledgeable practitioners, not exact replication by any reader. Proposals for public exchange and a future kit are prospects, while the described reuse within these projects is observed practice. Preserve this distinction in an abstract or contribution paragraph.

Useful transfer: show both how a practice emerged and what it makes understandable; let annotated material carry evidence; preserve failed paths and collaborators' roles; and distinguish reflective design knowledge from an evaluated procedure without diminishing its contribution.
