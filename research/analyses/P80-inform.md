## Knowledge object and architecture

The contribution combines an interaction design space—dynamic physical affordances and constraints—with a platform that makes those concepts concrete. The method is constructive implementation and demonstration, with technical characterization and informal feedback. These are not interchangeable contribution labels.

Actual order: **Introduction → Contributions → Related Work → Dynamic Affordances and Constraints → Demonstration Applications → Implementation → Discussion and Future Work → Conclusions → Acknowledgments → References**. The central conceptual section develops facilitating, restricting, and manipulating, then a parameter space. Applications come before the hardware implementation. Limitations are a subsection of Implementation, while interaction challenges recur in Discussion. There is no standalone controlled-study section.

## Abstract, introduction, and literature

The abstract begins with prior shape displays' representational emphasis, proposes three interaction roles, names the enabling system, and closes with motivating examples. The introduction (pp. 1–2) starts with everyday physical affordances, contrasts GUI dynamism with static tangible form, introduces dynamic affordances and constraints, extends them to passive-object manipulation, then presents the platform and roadmap. The contribution list distinguishes conceptual exploration, object actuation, system capability, and demonstrations.

Related Work (pp. 2–3) first clarifies theories of affordance, including differences among perceived, physical, and functional meanings. It then compares static tangibles, haptics, actuated objects, and dynamically controlled affordances. The synthesis returns to the token-and-constraint framework and explains what dynamic shape adaptation adds. Theory therefore supplies vocabulary for identifying the gap; it is not an ornamental citation block. This positioning also prevents the artifact's hardware novelty from carrying the whole argument.

## Evidence and boundaries

The central claim is an expanded interaction repertoire. Constructed wells, slots, handles, moving objects, and three applications instantiate the proposed categories. Measurements of actuation force, speed, refresh, and tracking characterize feasibility. Their warrant is that configurable surface geometry can change available actions and physically constrain motion.

The paper explicitly states that no formal quantitative user evaluation has yet occurred. Encounters with roughly 50 people supply observations about overlapping content and controls and jarring transitions; they do not establish efficiency, learnability, or general acceptance. Positive reactions cannot substitute for the missing comparative study. A 2.5D pin array also imposes geometry, cost, footprint, and object-motion constraints. Distinguish the demonstrated physical behavior from claims that it will reduce cognitive cost or improve guidance. Future general-purpose interfaces are a motivating trajectory rather than an observed deployment outcome.

## Transfer and exception

Introduce conceptual distinctions before enumerating hardware. Let demonstrations exercise different categories, and describe physical mechanisms after readers know why they matter. Mark the status of informal evidence at its point of use. This paper supports a concept–demonstration–implementation structure, with contributions outside a hypothesis-testing template. It does not justify omitting empirical validation when the intended claim concerns user performance.
