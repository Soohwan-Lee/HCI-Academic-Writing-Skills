## Contribution, evidence and method are separate

The introduction explicitly promises two outputs: a situated account of young
adults' sociotechnical routines under uncertainty, and implications for personal
informatics. Its main writing contract is **sociotechnical phenomenon interpretation**:
show how tools, peer commitments, records and cultural expectations participate
in constructing everyday well-being. The empirical material supports this account;
qualitative inquiry names how it is produced, not the entire intellectual contribution.

The study combines a workshop, a ten-day participant-authored mission phase and
follow-up peer-group interviews with 24 young adults. These activities create access
to accounts, written reflections and negotiations, while also shaping what becomes
available to analyze. The probe is not a randomized treatment or a demonstrated
well-being intervention. Participant reinterpretation during the probe cannot become
an independently measured improvement or a natural trajectory observed without
researcher involvement. The paper draws on situated/existential perspectives; this
is not automatically a new general theory or a formal extension of every cited model.

## Architecture and opening

Actual sequence: Abstract; Introduction; Background and Related Work (phenomenon,
cultural practices in HCI, PI for well-being, contemporary PI design); Method
(recruitment, probe design, analysis); Findings (interpretations/practices, negotiated
tensions, reinterpretation); Discussion (precarity, tensions, PI implications);
Limitations and Future Work; Conclusion; acknowledgments; references.

The opening moves from the meaning of well-being to routine and uncertainty, then
to a culturally situated phenomenon. It positions PI's behavioral emphasis against
an interpretive question, introduces three RQs and the access strategy, and states
findings and contributions. Its gap is an insufficient account of meaning and
social relations, not simply a missing feature or an unstudied national population.
The abstract likewise makes the explanatory tension visible before its design horizon.

Related work supplies four distinct dependencies. Local background makes the case
legible; cultural-practice scholarship establishes why situated inquiry can matter
beyond a location; PI scholarship identifies existing conceptual developments;
contemporary design work locates assumptions the account can reconsider. Section
2.3 acknowledges increasingly contextual PI models, so an assistant should not
compress the argument into a claim that all prior PI ignores social life.

## Analytical movement and limits

Findings first establish practices, then show competing demands and reinterpretations.
Discussion changes the level of explanation: relationships among records, obligations,
uncertainty and meanings become the central object. The same arrangements may
provide steadiness and impose pressure. That ambivalence is part of the explanation,
not noise to eliminate in a positive design story. Prospective design directions
follow this account but do not establish their own effectiveness.

The limits include purposive recruitment, self-described engagement, familiar-peer
pressures, a specific cultural moment and a short probe. No cross-cultural comparison
was performed. The interpretation should not become a statement about all Korean
young adults, cultural essence, or structural causality established by the sample.
The method disclaims exact perspective counts but later findings include aggregate
quantifiers; report that uneven practice rather than teaching an absolute ban on
numbers in interpretive research. A sampled successful paper is still open to critique.

## Two complete paragraph micro-analyses

**PDF p.8, §4.1.2 opening paragraph (three sentences).** S1 identifies participants'
assembly of arrangements rather than presenting a list of app preferences. S2
specifies the relationship between technical components and social/procedural ones,
then states their joint role. S3 previews the three patterns developed next. The
preceding §4.1.1 establishes meanings and variation; the next subsection shows how
intentions become organized routines. The sentence subjects maintain the same
analytic object while moving from actors' activity to its composition. The paragraph
works as a bridge from interpretation to enacted arrangements, not standalone proof
of its broadest claim.

**PDF p.12, §5.1 second paragraph (three sentences).** S1 proposes an analytical
name for the configuration in relation to uncertainty. S2 explains its composition
by contrasting individual effort with externally maintained obligations. S3 specifies
how commitments, routines and features relocate accountability. The prior paragraph
establishes the uncertainty participants described; the following paragraph develops
planning through an existing theoretical account. The new term therefore has a
job: compress an empirically developed relation and connect it to a wider argument.
An assistant should check the evidentiary bridge back to findings before reproducing
this move; naming an arrangement is not by itself theory generation.

## Transferable writing decision and counterexample

A sociotechnical findings section can develop **practice → relation → tension →
interpretation**, then use discussion to explain what familiar concepts miss. Keep
participant meanings, the analyst's interpretation and prospective design claims
attributed and distinct. This works when materials support relationships and context;
it should not impose structural critique on a descriptive usability study. Conversely,
requiring a new artifact, controlled benefit or feature checklist would misstate this
paper's contribution. P16, P33, P36 and P98 provide contrasting documentary, labor,
coordination and accessibility cases in the existing corpus, reducing dependence on
this one PI example.
