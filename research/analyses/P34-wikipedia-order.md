# P34 — The Work of Sustaining Order in Wikipedia

**Source and scope.** R. Stuart Geiger and David Ribes, *The Work of Sustaining Order in Wikipedia: The Banning of a Vandal*, CSCW 2010, DOI 10.1145/1718918.1718941, proceedings pp. 117–126. Read the ten-page [author-hosted copy](https://stuartgeiger.com/files/cscw-sustaining-order-wikipedia.pdf). Locators below use PDF pages because this copy lacks printed proceedings pagination. Selected through established infrastructure/ethnography scholars and the [author's publication record](https://stuartgeiger.com/articles/2010-02-25-cscw-banning-vandal/), not a verified award. This is single-analyst coding of that copy.

**Contribution versus technique.** The primary contribution is an interpretive account of vandal fighting as distributed cognition across people, software, and inscriptions. Trace ethnography is additionally presented and defended as a methodological contribution, with its own procedure, advantages, precedents, and limitations. This is more substantive than merely naming a method used to generate findings. Prior quantitative findings about tool prevalence motivate the inquiry; they are explicitly earlier work, not a new experiment performed here. The tools analyzed were not created as the paper's artifact contribution.

**Actual architecture and introduction.** Introduction → Wikipedia as a Problem of Order, with subsections on tools, trace ethnography, and distributed cognition → Case Study: The Banning of a Vandal, including A Vandal Emerges → Discussion (redistributing editing work; redistributing moral agency) → Conclusion. Literature, methodological explanation, and conceptual framing occupy connected subsections rather than separate conventional Related Work/Method headings. The abstract moves from empirical object and prior quantitative basis to method, interpretation, and explanatory implication. The introduction begins with a timestamped incident and reconstructed interface actions (PDF p. 1), then contrasts this process with explanations invoking a huge population of vigilant human reviewers. It identifies the object, explains tracing one user's blocking, and states the distributed-cognition thesis and insufficiency of purely social accounts (p. 2). This is an empirical puzzle opened through a vignette, not background-first abstraction or a numbered RQ list.

**Related-work synthesis.** The problem-of-order discussion distinguishes accounts of norms/governance, proposed tools, detection algorithms, and older descriptions of actual editing. Their shared inadequacy is explanatory: they do not show how current unofficial tools transform work. Earlier prevalence evidence makes this omission consequential. Hutchins supplies an analogy and a concept, but the paper also names the difference between a professional ship crew and a decentralized volunteer network. Trace-ethnography precedents establish why logs need situated knowledge rather than treating logs as self-explanatory behavior.

**Claim–support–warrant–limit.** The case follows edits, reversions, warning levels, tool-generated messages, a report, and blocking. The warrant for distributed cognition is not simply that many actors participated: earlier judgments become reusable inscriptions that subsequent actors and tools combine into a decision. The discussion shows how interfaces make suspicious changes perceptible and redistribute decision-making. The moral-agency section qualifies technological determinism: users can depart from defaults, but doing so requires effort. The conclusion explicitly limits trace ethnography's access to broader history and sociocultural significance and proposes complementary methods. One reconstructed episode does not estimate all vandalism work or prove current Wikipedia behavior. A simulated interface figure must not be treated as a contemporaneous captured screen.

**Micro-analysis.** Visually verified PDF p. 9, Discussion, opening “One of the most”: five sentences. S1 identifies transformed reviewing decisions as the topic; S2 specifies the decisions' role in legitimizing content and contributors; S3 interprets inclusion/exclusion as moral action; S4 derives a need for scrutiny; S5 concludes that describing automation as mere acceleration is inadequate and risky. Relations: topical claim → contextual specification → conceptual warrant → consequence → sharpened conclusion. Thus the opener is not yet the paragraph's full argumentative conclusion. The previous paragraph closes the infrastructure argument; the next gives algorithmic examples, including differential treatment of anonymous/new editors. The paragraph converts a descriptive account into a normative question before supporting it concretely.

**Transfer and exception.** A well-chosen episode can establish an explanatory puzzle and later become the case used to resolve it. Show how each trace licenses the reconstruction and why the theoretical interpretation exceeds a chronological retelling. This pattern does not replace sampling and comparative evidence where the claim concerns prevalence or average effects. Avoid mechanically adding separate Method and Related Work headings if their actual function is an integrated conceptual-methodological setup. No universal prose-length rule is inferred.
