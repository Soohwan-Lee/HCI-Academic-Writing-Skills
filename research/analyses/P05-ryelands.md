## Architecture and rhetorical moves

The sequence is introduction → related work → methodology → exploratory study → findings and educational propositions → discussion → conclusion. Methodology covers RtD, context, ethics, analysis, and positionality. The study account separates pre-deployment, classroom deployment, and post-deployment activities while explicitly warning that those phases overlap and were identified retrospectively.

The abstract (p. 1) presents a societal/educational demand, the school partnership and RtD approach, a principal observed relationship between competencies, educational guidance, and wider design/methodological implications. The introduction (§1, pp. 1–2) moves from technological change and uncertainty to an unusually early statement of epistemological commitments. It then situates education, identifies limited sustained engagement with actual technology, explains the constructionist curriculum, and previews contributions. It explicitly frames the knowledge as contingent and generative rather than generalizable theory. The thing being designed is the curriculum as a whole, not merely an image-generation interface.

## Related-work argument

Section 2 (pp. 2–3) first delimits the literature: HCI for education, rather than education about HCI. Constructionism supplies the learning rationale; AI-literacy work supplies the domain context; design-led AI inquiries establish methodological precedents. The synthesis identifies a conjunction that is insufficiently addressed: educator involvement, actual technology use, and prolonged engagement on a qualitative footing. That conjunction motivates the methodology in §3. The gap is therefore an opportunity for situated inquiry, not simply a missing randomized comparison.

## Claim, evidence, and warrant

The argument rests on a documented six-week design/deployment process, evolving materials, student outputs and responses, researcher reflection, and teacher interviews (§§3–5). Section 5 places findings next to the propositions developed from them. The discussion then extends from educational implications to broader design strategies and RtD's responsiveness (§6).

The warrant is interpretive and generative: documented making and situated observations can inform subsequent inquiry. They do not establish a treatment effect. Section 3.1 explicitly contrasts the chosen approach with a control-group evaluation, and §6.3 acknowledges a single school/context and possible novelty effects. Section 5.2.2 is especially useful rhetorically because it identifies a missed opportunity in the researchers' own intervention and develops a design proposition from that limitation. This gives reflexivity argumentative work rather than treating it as a generic declaration.

## Transferable writing decisions

- Explain the intended form of knowledge early enough that readers can judge the evidence appropriately.
- Identify the unit of design and preserve the distinction between study chronology and retrospective analytical structure.
- Link concrete design changes and incidents to interpretations, then to bounded propositions.
- Separate domain guidance from the methodological claim and state the evidence limits of each.

**Counterexample to a rigid template:** numbered RQs, a fixed intervention, and a detached results section are not necessary for this RtD account. Its combined findings/propositions section is intentional. Conversely, merely building a prototype and interviewing users would not automatically justify the RtD label or this structure.

## Selected paragraph: sentence-level flow

**Locator:** §1, PDF p. 1, opening “In this paper, we adopt”. Four sentences: **S1** states the methodological response and exploratory scope; **S2** distinguishes the chosen emphasis from artifact-centered RtD examples; **S3** explains the epistemic consequence: contingent, generative usefulness; **S4** translates that commitment into the contribution's boundary and place in a research program. The relation is **approach → distinguishing commitment → consequence → contribution contract**. It follows a paragraph about public and scholarly uncertainty; it precedes the educational context, so the reader receives the interpretive contract before the particular study setting. This early scope-setting paragraph is deductive. By contrast, §5.1.1 builds part of its reasoning through classroom incidents before stating their significance; that evidential passage should not be mechanically rewritten as a sequence of front-loaded conclusions.
