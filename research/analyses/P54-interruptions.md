# P54 — The Cost of Interrupted Work

Mark, Gudith and Klocke, CHI 2008. [Author-hosted PDF](https://www.ics.uci.edu/~gmark/chi08-mark.pdf).
Single-analyst full-text reading; four-page proceedings-style copy. This is a
short-format historical empirical exemplar, not an 8,000-word allocation model.
Affiliations: UC Irvine and Humboldt University. Award unverified.

## Knowledge object and architecture
The contribution is an empirical account of interruption costs and trade-offs,
not the office simulator itself. A controlled laboratory task supplies the warrant;
personality measures are associated with outcomes, not randomized treatments.
The sequence is Introduction → Interruptions and Context → Experimental Design
and Procedure → Results → Discussion and Conclusions. The introduction proper is
one paragraph; much of the motivation and literature positioning occurs under the
next heading. Counting only the literal Introduction would misrepresent the
rhetorical introduction. There is no stand-alone Related Work or numbered RQ list.

The abstract opens directly with the study question, then reports the unexpected
performance pattern, proposes an interpretation with a cost, mentions individual
differences, and ends with design relevance. It omits a long global background.
The opening section moves from prior investigation to conflicting findings.
The contextual section considers competing accounts: interruption context may
help, interfere, or be irrelevant. It then broadens the outcome beyond elapsed
time to workload and individual differences. The laboratory design follows from
those distinctions. This is a valuable alternative to a gap framed only as absence.

## Evidence and positioning
The study distinguishes task completion time from time spent handling the
interruptions, and describes that operationalization before presenting results
(p. 2). The Results section follows the questions and checks alternative accounts,
including message length and actual interruption duration (p. 3). Its tables
include several outcomes; faster completion is not treated as an unqualified win.
The discussion returns to earlier literature by distinguishing content similarity
from similarity of cognitive operations. That is a construct-level reconciliation,
not a claim that earlier studies were simply wrong.

The proposed compensatory strategy is explicitly an interpretation. The authors
also propose another explanation and state that finer-grained measurements would
be required (p. 4). Laboratory control is defended while ecological limitations
are acknowledged. Long-term coping is left unresolved. For modern writing, retain
this distinction between result and explanation; do not inherit the paper's
strong null-result wording as proof of equivalence. A nonsignificant context
difference does not establish that context never matters. Likewise, system
customization is a design implication, not a tested intervention here.

## Micro-analysis 1: result to interpretation to cost
Discussion, p. 4, second paragraph; opening locator “Surprisingly our results show”.
Five sentences, visually checked. S1 gives the unexpected measured pattern. S2
explicitly announces an interpretive shift. S3 supplies a possible compensatory
mechanism. S4 contrasts speed with the observed workload costs. S5 compresses
those two outcomes into a bounded takeaway. Sequence: **observation → epistemic
signpost → explanation → counterweight → synthesis**. The preceding paragraph
reconciles the context result with prior studies; the following one expands
alternative mechanisms and states what evidence is missing. The paragraph is
claim-first about an observation, not proof-first about a mechanism.

## Micro-analysis 2: implication and boundary in one paragraph
Discussion, p. 4, last paragraph, opening “Our results have implications”. Nine
sentences, continuing across columns. S1 signals a change of purpose. S2 proposes
a tolerability inference. S3 suggests a longitudinal management capability with
reference to workload. S4 returns to the short observed exposure. S5 explicitly
withholds a long-term conclusion. S6 connects the laboratory result to field
reports. S7 identifies the individual-difference result. S8 proposes customization.
S9 invites follow-up. Its useful feature is the return to evidence and temporal
limits inside the implication, rather than placing all qualifications elsewhere.
Its generic closing invitation is dispensable in a compressed contemporary draft.

## Transfer to manuscript editing
Make competing explanations explicit before naming a factor. State precisely what
timing excludes. Pair benefit with measured cost. Mark proposed mechanisms as
interpretations and identify a discriminating future measurement. Do not turn a
four-page precedent into a universal section ratio, copy old statistical practices
without checking their appropriateness, or inflate a nonsignificant result into
absence of an effect. These are writing judgments from one exemplar, not a causal
account of the paper's reception.
