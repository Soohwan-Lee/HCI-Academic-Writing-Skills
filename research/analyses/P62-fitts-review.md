# P62 — A Systematic Review of Fitts’ Law in 3D Extended Reality

Amini, Stuerzlinger, Teather and Batmaz, CHI 2025; Concordia University, Simon
Fraser University and Carleton University. [Coauthor-hosted PDF](https://www.csit.carleton.ca/~rteather/pdfs/CHI2025.pdf),
DOI 10.1145/3706598.3713623. Twenty-five-page proceedings copy; substantive text
and recommendation appendices read, PDF p. 5 rendered. Award unverified.

## Contribution and architecture
The contribution is a systematic synthesis of methodological practice and a
proposed reporting/design framework. The analyzed units are 119 publications
containing 122 eligible studies, not 122 participants. Although the authors call
parts of their work meta-analysis, the reported synthesis primarily describes
practices, distributions and ranges; it is not a pooled treatment-effect estimate
with a reported inverse-variance model. A writing skill should describe the actual
analysis rather than infer its properties from that label.

Order: Introduction → Related Work → Motivation and Objectives → Methodology →
Results → Discussion → Conclusions → references → model summary and recommended
framework appendices. Results group model variants, applications, environments,
measures, task design and participants. Discussion changes purpose to comparability,
interpretation, recommendations and open problems. Appendices turn the argument
into an inspectable resource organized around research goals and study stages.

The abstract moves from widespread use to variation despite standards, the review
scope, a broad finding and a promised framework. Introduction develops the
historical move from simpler pointing to XR and why that complicates comparison.
Related Work explains models and neighboring reviews before a separate objectives
section states the synthesis tasks. This is a knowledge prerequisite: readers
need to understand what can vary before evaluating the extraction scheme. It is
not evidence that every review needs three long motivation sections.

## Review method and limits of the warrant
The paper reports database-specific queries, search date, selection stages,
access failures, eligibility and separate publication/study counts. It distinguishes
papers mentioning a method from studies applying it. Detailed extraction excludes
some results that cannot be converted reliably. It also discusses screened-out
applications more broadly; those contextual examples must not be silently added
to the detailed-analysis denominator.

Explicit limitations include single-author screening, manual duplicate management,
possible temporal coding inconsistency, reliance on published values and lack of
within-study risk-of-bias appraisal. Naming PRISMA does not remove those limits,
and reporting-guideline compliance is not proof of validity. Peer review of source
papers is not a substitute for examining their biases. The review's assertions
that its protocol ensures reliability are stronger than those facts establish.

Cross-study clusters reveal reporting variation but cannot isolate effects of
feedback, display or other factors when designs differ. Recommendations that
follow directly from missing information—state units, formula and operational
choices—have a clearer warrant than prescribing a sample size or difficulty range
because it is common. The appendices sometimes make that latter move. Preserve
it as an attributable author recommendation, not a validated norm. Likewise,
outlying performance relative to the corpus calls for contextual examination,
not automatic rejection of a new result. This is a methodological contrast as
well as a useful organizational exemplar.

## Micro-analysis A: expose extraction precision
PDF p. 5, §4.3 first paragraph, locator “For analyzing Fitts’ methodology”. Five
sentences. S1 states the extraction purpose. S2 points to the variable schema.
S3 limits chart extraction to ranges supported by axis granularity. S4 explains
manual extraction and checking as a response to varied presentation. S5 explains
two cycles: discovery/organization followed by synthesis formatting. Flow:
**goal → schema → precision boundary → procedure rationale → staged workflow**.
It follows study inclusion and precedes terminology harmonization. The paragraph
makes data production inspectable instead of treating a review spreadsheet as
an already-clean source.

## Micro-analysis B: distinguish labels from constructs
Same page, §4.3 second paragraph, locator “We also observed varying”. Five
sentences. S1 identifies inconsistent terminology. S2 states when differently
named values are merged. S3 states the converse rule for similar names measuring
different things. S4 illustrates an excluded conversion. S5 applies the distinction
to movement versus total task time. Flow: **problem → inclusion rule → exclusion
rule → example → second example with purpose**. The following subsection discusses
bias. Cohesion depends on a paired distinction carried through examples, not
simply adding transition words between otherwise unrelated sentences.

## Transfer and exceptions
A review's contribution must be more than a flow diagram and counts. Connect
extraction decisions to synthesis comparability, show what the resulting map
reveals, and justify recommendations separately. Report uncertainty and unavailable
information rather than converting graph readings into exact numbers. Do not
import the paper's methodological prescriptions or present descriptive aggregation
as a causal meta-analysis. A critical conceptual review needs transparent selection
and interpretation too, but need not mimic this database-driven structure.
