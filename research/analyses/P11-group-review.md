# P11 — A Systematic Review of Interaction Design Strategies for Group Recommendation Systems

**Source:** Alvarado, Htun, Jin, and Verbert, PACMHCI 6(CSCW2), 2022, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3555161), [author copy](https://yucheng.gold/file/cscw22.pdf). Single-analyst rhetorical coding of this PDF; introduction page 2 was visually checked. This is a 51-page article, not an 8,000-word template. Awards are not inferred.

## Contribution and architecture

Primary contribution: literature synthesis and an interaction-design typology. Descriptive counting and thematic interpretation are analytical methods; the paper does not contribute a newly implemented group recommender. Although the authors use the term meta-analysis, the reported analysis maps and synthesizes design features rather than pooling standardized intervention effects (§2.6).

The actual top-level sequence, with long labels paraphrased, is §1 Introduction → §2 Methodology → §3 review findings → §4 design typology and research opportunities → §5 Limitations, followed by acknowledgments, references, and appendices. There is no standalone Related Work or Conclusion. Findings already include local interpretation; §4 consolidates rather than introducing interpretation for the first time.

## Abstract and introduction moves

The abstract (p. 1) establishes the application context, identifies adverse user experiences, proposes a synthesis, specifies retrieval and analysis, outlines analytical dimensions, and announces the typology and opportunities. The introduction (§1, pp. 1–3) narrows everyday recommendation to group situations through familiar joint activities. It explains why group interaction creates distinct design demands, connects these to previously identified problems, and acknowledges earlier surveys before identifying a missing human-centered synthesis. Six research questions precede the corpus-selection overview. The final move states the typology and community-facing research agenda. Here an explicit RQ sequence is functional because it organizes the subsequent review dimensions.

## Positioning and evidence

The introduction performs the comparative work usually assigned to Related Work: prior surveys cover other aspects, while this review centers interaction strategies. The methods make the object of inference concrete through eligibility, databases, queries and dates, selection, coding, and analysis (§§2.1–2.6). The selection flow and coding account are therefore part of the contribution's credibility, not administrative detail.

The claim–evidence–warrant chain runs from a bounded set of interface-bearing publications to coded features and qualitative categories, then to a typology and proposed research opportunities (§§3–4). Tables link categories back to papers; illustrated interfaces make otherwise abstract design categories inspectable. The warrant for an opportunity is an observed concentration or omission plus an explanation of why an alternative matters. Sparse coverage alone does not establish that an unexplored feature will improve outcomes.

Limitations (§5) concern database scope, manual errors, and inclusion regardless of study quality. English-only eligibility and the dated search (§2) also constrain generalization. Descriptions of published artifacts should not be converted into claims about all deployed systems or comparative effectiveness.

## Transferable writing decisions

Keep RQs, coding fields, findings, and synthesis aligned. Use a table to retain the evidence trail while prose explains a pattern. Separate descriptive coverage from evaluative judgment and distinguish an agenda from validated recommendations. Make the review's exclusions legible so absence claims have a denominator. For an 8,000-word manuscript, move detailed inventories to supplements only if the main argument remains inspectable; do not mechanically preserve this article's page proportions.

This example demonstrates that related-work positioning can occur without a named section and that a review can end with limits rather than a separate conclusion. These observations are not prescriptions to omit either section. No numerical style norm is inferred from this single article.

## Paragraph micro-analysis

**Locator:** §1, p. 2, opening “Extensive academic work”. Three sentences (visually checked): **S1** acknowledges existing surveys across several technical and application dimensions (concession and scope of established knowledge); **S2** specifies the absent synthesis by stance and interaction dimensions, qualified by the authors' knowledge (contrast and bounded gap claim); **S3** explains why that synthesis would help designers and practitioners (significance and bridge). The first sentence is a topic-setting acknowledgment, not the paragraph's main conclusion: the argumentative center arrives in S2. The preceding paragraph establishes group-specific design challenges and adverse experiences. This paragraph shows why existing literature does not yet resolve that need. The following paragraph operationalizes the gap into six research questions. The sequence is need → nearest work and unresolved synthesis → questions, with continuity provided by repeated analytical dimensions rather than generic transition phrases. A writing skill should preserve the qualification and verify the coverage claim rather than automatically generating a “no prior work” sentence.
