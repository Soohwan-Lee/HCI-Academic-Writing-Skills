# P09 — Time for Historicism in CSCW: An Invitation

**Source:** Soden, Ribes, Avle, and Sutherland, PACMHCI 5(CSCW2), 2021, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3479603), [author copy](https://robertsoden.io/files/historicsm-cscw.pdf). Single-analyst rhetorical coding of this version; page-one layout was visually checked. Locators below use article pages. Awards require separate verification.

## Contribution and architecture

Primary contribution: a conceptual and programmatic argument for historical attentiveness in CSCW. A secondary contribution is methodological orientation. This is neither a newly conducted historical case study nor a systematic literature review; selected literature and examples develop an argument.

The top-level order, paraphrasing long headings, is §1 Introduction → §2 problems caused by present-centered thinking → §3 existing CSCW connections to historical inquiry → §4 proposed historical orientation → §5 Conclusion. Section 4 alternates three substantive propositions with their corresponding cautions. There are no separate Related Work, Methods, Results, or Discussion sections. That absence follows the contribution rather than indicating missing empirical reporting.

## Abstract and introduction moves

The abstract (p. 1) identifies an underused orientation, diagnoses what the field's future-facing emphasis loses, states a normative thesis, and previews intellectual connections and methodological challenges. It does not promise a treatment effect or empirical finding.

The introduction (§1, pp. 1–3) begins immediately with the authors' position. It defines the proposed orientation and its intended reach before establishing contemporary urgency through CSCW's concern with justice, exclusions, and environmental consequences. It then identifies the unresolved problem: previous historical work exists, but the field lacks a sustained shared conversation about presentism. The introduction locates the argument in neighboring traditions while explaining why CSCW needs its own formulation. It closes with themes, a roadmap, and an explicit statement of provisionality. Thus claim → explanation → stakes → gap → intellectual approach → qualified contribution is a plausible alternative to background-first writing.

## Related work and argumentative support

Section 3 connects coordination, infrastructure/repair, politics, environment, and reflexive practice to the proposed orientation. Those literatures supply footholds and continuity rather than a list of studies to dismiss. The authors identify their own intellectual positioning and deny comprehensiveness. Section 4 then draws on historical examples and theoretical traditions to substantiate each proposition while making alternative readings and archival difficulties visible.

The claim–evidence–warrant chain is a proposed way of investigating technology, supported by examples showing what historical interpretation reveals, with the inference that CSCW questions can gain depth through that orientation. This is an argued benefit, not a comparative evaluation proving that historical framing improves every study. Counterarguments remain beside the claims: incomplete archives, dominant record keepers, contested accounts of progress, and instrumental treatment of past lives qualify the proposed benefits (§§4.1.1–4.3.1).

## Transferable writing decisions

An argument paper can state its thesis before surveying context. Explain what the lens changes in a reader's reasoning, then demonstrate this through selected cases. Position prior work as intellectual infrastructure. Pair an opportunity with its difficult boundary rather than postponing every qualification to a generic limitations paragraph. Conclude by sharpening the invitation and unresolved stakes, without inventing an empirical validation.

This case supports a conceptual route distinct from an empirical template. It does not justify vague opinion: definitions, examples, citations, and engagement with objections do the evidential work. The writing strategy is an editorial interpretation of this paper, not an established success formula, and no quantitative length norms are inferred.

## Paragraph micro-analysis

**Locator:** §1, p. 2, opening “We consider such an orientation”. Five sentences (visually checked): **S1** asserts urgency and connects it to current social/environmental concerns (local evaluative claim); **S2** concedes that these concerns have longstanding roots while identifying a renewed emphasis on ethics and exclusions (qualification, then contextual evidence); **S3** supplies concrete research areas supporting that emphasis (examples with citations); **S4** broadens the evidence to economic, environmental, and disaster contexts (extension); **S5** adds scrutiny of technology-industry narratives (convergence). This is a deductive paragraph at the local argumentative level: it announces why the proposed orientation matters before supplying support. It does not summarize a newly measured result. The preceding paragraph defines the orientation and its benefits; this paragraph answers why it matters now. The following paragraph turns urgency into a diagnosis of presentism, then identifies the missing sustained conversation. The chain is definition → urgency → diagnosis, rather than repeated claim-and-summary paragraphs.
