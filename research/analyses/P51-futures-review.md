# P51 — Let’s Talk Futures: A Literature Review of HCI’s Future-Orientation

**Identity.** Sanchez, Wang, Savolainen, Epp, and Salovaara, CHI 2025, DOI 10.1145/3706598.3713759. Analyzed arXiv:2502.09362v1, dated 13 February 2025: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.09362v1. Single-analyst writing analysis.

## Architecture and opening

Actual sequence: Introduction → Related Research (conventional approaches; speculative futures; possible futures; futures studies) → A Literature-Based Examination of Futuring in HCI (identification; screening; qualitative analysis) → Findings (RQ1 development; RQ2 characteristics; summary) → Discussion (expanding beyond technology; scaling HCI strengths) → Conclusion → references and appendices with selection criteria and corpus material. This is a structured review with systematic retrieval/screening and interpretive synthesis, not a meta-analysis of intervention effects.

The abstract moves from HCI’s future orientation to shortcomings, review scope, development of SPIN, mixed findings, and five opportunities. Introduction illustrates the problem through scenario construction, connects it to systemic consequences and excluded perspectives, recognizes emerging alternatives, and then states two RQs: temporal development and traits of comprehensive futuring. A procedural preview links those questions to screening and full-text interpretation. Related Research establishes the concepts and the reason to inspect publications that do not explicitly identify as futures research. Its final move distinguishes growing interest from knowledge of actual uptake; this warrants the review rather than substituting for it.

## Review warrant and bounded claims

The retrieval covers eleven ACM outlets and research-article records from 2008–2023, combining future terms with STEEPLE-related terms. Citation filters reduce 11,587 records to 2,621 for screening; 259 are classified as fleeting futuring and 205 as comprehensive. Seven calibration rounds revise criteria; the paper reports agreement for that classification task. The later in-depth analysis of 205 papers uses an evolving, futures-studies-informed framework and abductive interpretation. These are different analytical operations: screening reliability should not be reported as proof that interpretive themes are objectively true.

SPIN organizes epistemic stance, contingency perceptions, systemic integration, and narrative. The account explains how initial full readings exposed limitations in imported typologies, prompting attention to individual experience, culture, and more-than-human relations. Findings use examples and quotations to connect corpus-level patterns to actual representations of futures; Discussion derives opportunities from both shortcomings and distinctive strengths. The primary warrant is an inspectable relation between corpus construction, coding lenses, exemplars, and synthesis. The framework is an analytical contribution, not a separately evaluated intervention.

The paper explicitly limits representativeness because citation-based selection favors influential papers and does not cover all futuring research. Its temporal graph is counts within that selected corpus, not all HCI output or normalized prevalence. A writing skill should preserve this qualification near trend claims and avoid upgrading broad concluding language into population estimates. Likewise, method transparency and a PRISMA-style diagram do not by themselves establish exhaustive coverage. The preprint contains rough wording and no separately titled Limitations section; quality selection does not make every sentence a model to imitate.

## Transfer and exception

Separate a review’s background literature from the corpus analyzed as evidence, and explain how each search/filter choice serves the question. Make framework changes visible when data exceed a borrowed lens. Link descriptive findings to interpretive findings without collapsing their evidence standards. This paper supports a review-writing route with retrieval, selection, coding, synthesis, scope limits, and traceable agenda proposals; it does not supply a universal protocol for every critical essay, scoping review, or meta-analysis.
