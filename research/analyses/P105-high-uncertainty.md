## Contribution and structure

The contribution is empirical knowledge about explanation style, AI confidence, and correctness in novice decisions. The trading interface and random-forest models create the experimental setting; they are not claimed as a novel forecasting algorithm. Inductive, abductive, and deductive labels refer to concrete example-based, SHAP-based, and rule-based explanations. Results therefore concern these implementations in this task rather than every possible realization of those reasoning traditions.

The section sequence is Introduction; Related Work; Study Design (materials, procedure, hypotheses, analytical approaches); Results (participants and H 1–H 3); Discussion (implications, limitations); Conclusion; acknowledgments; references. Hypotheses follow detailed materials and procedure. Results mirror reliance rankings, task performance, and agreement, while a discussion table records expected versus observed outcomes. The table marks H 1 b as not matching its predicted ranking: do not summarize all hypotheses as uniformly supported.

The abstract moves from appropriate-trust objective to known overreliance, difficult-task motivation, study factors/outcomes, conditional results, and bounded design implications. Introduction pp 251–252 builds through contradictory explanation findings, confidence and correctness, underexamined presentation/reasoning, the interaction question, study design, results, and two contributions. ## Evidence and warrants

The online study reports 184 nonexpert participants and 734 analyzed task instances, from approximately 250 initially recruited participants. Each participant sees four explanation conditions, including no explanation, with buying/selling tasks and confidence/correctness balanced. The manuscript has some ambiguous instance-selection and exclusion wording; those counts should be retained separately rather than silently converted to a complete rectangular dataset.

Reliance is a self-reported ranking of interface information, not observed advice acceptance. Agreement is matching the AI decision, and performance is correctness against the task outcome. The distinction allows agreement to mean something different when advice is wrong. Conditional findings favor abductive/deductive implementations at high confidence; low-confidence recommendations differ. A claim about interaction must retain the compared conditions, rather than becoming “explanations improve trust.” Tables give regression coefficients and reference levels; using them as direct pairwise superiority evidence requires the appropriate contrast, not just a significant interaction coefficient.

The inference from interface conditions to human reasoning is partly interpretive: the task does not directly observe a participant executing a particular logical process. Likewise, statistically nonsignificant differences do not establish identical reliance. Limits include domain, selected stocks/time windows, indicators, novice sample, artificial stakes, explanation-generation technique, and the confidence estimator and cutoff. Repeated decisions by people also make unit/dependence reporting important. The exact stock choices were conditioned on model performance; their use is not an unqualified random sample of financial decisions.

## Two paragraph micro-analyses

**Printed p 261/PDF 12, §5.1, “We registered all the” (eight body sentences, excluding italic recommendation label).** S 1 locates the effective styles; S 2 identifies their significance for XAI; S 3 proposes reasoning fit as the mechanism; S 4 specifies tabular time-series data; S 5 contrasts its demands with other tasks; S 6 diagnoses example-based effort; S 7 contrasts interpretive support from the other styles; S 8 connects that account to decisions. The prior paragraph recommends confidence-sensitive explanation presentation; the next qualifies the task/data dependence. This is result-first explanation, but several mechanism sentences exceed directly measured behavior and should remain interpretive.

**Same page/section, “We believe that this” (three sentences).** S 1 bounds the effect by task and data; S 2 predicts a different result for easier image-classification tasks; S 3 explains why visual comparison might lower effort. It follows the mechanism paragraph and precedes the AI-correctness implication. This is qualification-first: a boundary generates a future hypothesis instead of merely adding generic limitations. The cross-domain prediction is not evidence from a second experiment.

**Transfer.** Define related constructs separately, align conditional hypotheses with conditional summaries, and distinguish category labels from implementations. A hypothesis table can expose exceptions more clearly than a celebratory results recap.
