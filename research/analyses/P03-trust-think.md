# P03 — To Trust or to Think

Buçinca, Malaya, and Gajos. CSCW1 2021, article 188.
[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/3449287) · [Analyzed author PDF](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2021/bucinca21trust.pdf).

## Contribution and evidence

Primary route: quantitative empirical. Interaction-design interventions are the tested
means of producing the knowledge claim; this note does not assign a separate artifact
contribution merely because interfaces were built.
The knowledge claim concerns conditions under which cognitive forcing affects reliance,
performance, and acceptance; the interfaces are experimental treatments, not a standalone
system contribution. A controlled nutrition task with a simulated AI supports comparisons
between intervention categories. The design is mixed within/between subjects, and the
analysis accounts for repeated observations (Section 3.6, pp. 9–10).

## Observed architecture

Introduction → related work → experiment → results → ethical considerations through
an individual-differences analysis → discussion → conclusion. The internal audit is a
substantial analytical section, not a boilerplate ethics paragraph. Limitations occur
within the discussion. Formal hypotheses appear at the beginning of the experiment,
after their conceptual motivation in the introduction and related work.

## Opening moves

The abstract compresses problem, failure of an existing remedy, theoretical explanation,
intervention, experiment, conditional improvement, acceptability trade-off, inequality
audit, and broader implication. It reports an adverse consequence as part of the main
message rather than reserving all qualification for the discussion.

The introduction (pp. 1–3) starts with applications and an aspiration–outcome mismatch.
It then explains why explanations have not resolved overreliance, introduces dual-process
theory, diagnoses the assumption of analytical engagement, and proposes a change in
interaction. It motivates interventions and an anticipated trade-off before describing
the experiment. Findings, residual failure, and individual differences precede two
explicit contributions. This is a theory-to-intervention argument; inserting a separate
RQ list before the approach would not reproduce the actual structure.

## Paragraph and sentence flow

Introduction, p. 2, opening locator **“We posit that”**: S1 proposes a theoretical lens;
S2 defines its fast-process account; S3 explains the cost of deliberate processing;
S4 identifies the resulting vulnerability; S5 qualifies the negative reading of fast
processing; S6 supplies expertise as a counterexample; S7 argues for combining modes.
The relations are claim → explanation → mechanism → consequence → qualification →
example → synthesis. S1 is a topic-and-position sentence, not a study-result conclusion.
The preceding paragraph reports the limitations of explanations; the next applies the
lens to the assumption that users inspect each explanation. The paragraph therefore
builds the warrant needed for that next diagnostic step rather than merely expanding
a broad background topic.

## Related-work positioning

Section 2 first distinguishes educational debiasing from intervention during a decision.
It then connects overreliance findings to concrete candidate interactions: deciding first,
delaying advice, and requesting advice. Prior work performs two jobs: explaining a
mechanism and justifying treatment choices. The final discussion of effort and preference
sets up the acceptability hypothesis. A transferable device is to connect each literature
strand to a subsequent design or measurement decision, rather than ending every subsection
with a generic assertion that research is missing.

## Claim, warrant, and boundary

Sections 4 and 6 distinguish performance across all trials from performance when AI is
wrong. The latter comparison supports a narrower benefit; the overall-performance
hypothesis is not supported. The paper's broad summary should therefore not be converted
into a claim that every forcing design improves every measure. Section 5 disaggregates
results by Need for Cognition; differing within-group significance alone should not be
rewritten as a newly demonstrated interaction effect. The authors acknowledge a single,
non-critical task and the possibility that perceived usability affects adoption (p. 17).

## Writing moves worth transferring

- Introduce the failed assumption between the existing remedy and the new intervention.
- Present positive and null outcomes together, tied to their respective hypotheses.
- Separate related constructs: liking, trust, effort, reliance, and performance.
- Give a consequential secondary analysis its own section when it changes the contribution.
- Treat future adaptive designs as proposals, not evaluated capabilities.

These are single-analyst interpretations of the linked PDF, not estimates of prevalence
or evidence that this structure causes acceptance. No paragraph-length norm is inferred.
