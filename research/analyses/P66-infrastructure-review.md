## Architecture and opening

Actual sequence: Introduction → Methodology (Data Collection; Data Analysis) → Descriptive Statistics → Findings (Growing; Appropriating; Coping with Infrastructure) → Discussion (Informal Activities; Problematizing Infrastructure) → Limitations → Conclusions → references and corpus appendices. There is no separate Related Work: the introduction locates infrastructure theory, its adoption across fields, and existing domain-specific reviews before establishing the need for a SIGCHI synthesis.

The abstract gives the topic, review method and scope, theoretical lineage, three themes, and critical implication. Introduction begins with familiar infrastructures, then changes the analytical object from physical systems to relational arrangements by explaining Star and Ruhleder’s framework. Examples across subfields establish breadth; existing reviews provide both precedents and interpretive resources. The last introduction paragraphs preview findings and explicitly distinguish corpus documentation, thematic synthesis, and implications as contributions. This sequence gives readers the conceptual knowledge required to understand what counts as an eligible infrastructure paper.

## Claim, support, warrant, and limits

The review includes studies that explicitly use infrastructure as a sociotechnical concept and examine interaction with populations. The reported flow proceeds from 625 records to 342 after format/language exclusions, to 165 conceptually relevant papers, then adds 25 through snowballing. The thematic analysis codes conceptual contributions and permits overlap among themes. Counts describe the corpus; examples supply the conceptual detail through which categories become meaningful. The distinction between repairing infrastructure and coping with its failures depends on analytical perspective, not simply the presence of a breakdown.

The critical contribution extends from describing beneficial infrastructure to examining arrangements that constrain, exclude, or intentionally harm. Its warrant is cross-study interpretation of membership, power, and purpose, rather than evidence that a particular design intervention prevents harm. Discussion also treats invisible work as situated: invisibility can obscure contributions but can sometimes be valued by insiders. That contrast avoids converting visibility into an unconditional design recommendation.

This preprint is useful partly as a cautionary case. Its conclusion reports 174 studies despite 190 elsewhere; collection says before 2024 while other passages include 2024. These discrepancies must not be silently resolved into exact corpus facts. The paper argues for SIGCHI’s relevance but cannot establish statistical representativeness by venue prestige. Keyword and venue restrictions are acknowledged, and some relevant external studies enter discussion rather than the counted corpus. A derivative skill should distinguish those roles and demand a final consistency check. No independent coding agreement should be inferred from the word “systematic.”

## Two paragraph micro-analyses

**PDF p. 16, Discussion §5.2, “In conclusion, we encourage” (five sentences; rendered).** S1 states the recommended research emphasis. S2 supplies the critical premise that infrastructures need not benefit everyone. S3 derives ongoing examination of their purposes. S4 identifies social-justice issues as an analytical resource. S5 names participatory design and ethnography as possible approaches. Relations: recommendation → rationale → action → analytical focus → methodological route. The preceding paragraph discusses ethnographic examples; the following section turns to limitations. This is recommendation-first, with support accumulated earlier rather than newly demonstrated inside the paragraph.

**PDF p. 17, Limitations, “We are aware that” (three sentences; rendered).** S1 acknowledges possible missing literature and its consequence. S2 describes two mitigations: snowballing and contextual engagement with relevant excluded work. S3 explains their intended balance between corpus focus and broader discussion. Relations: coverage limitation → mitigation → bounded rationale. The preceding paragraph explains keyword restrictions; Conclusions follows. The mitigation does not erase the selection boundary or authorize counting background studies as primary studies.

## Transfer

A review can separate descriptive maps from interpretive themes and critical implications. Explain category overlaps and perspectives, and keep an explicit distinction between analyzed corpus and contextual scholarship. Use this manuscript’s count and date discrepancies as editing checks, not examples to reproduce; strong provenance does not make every version’s prose or numbers exemplary.
