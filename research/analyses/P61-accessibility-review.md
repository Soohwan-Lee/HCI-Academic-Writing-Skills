# P61 — What Do We Mean by “Accessibility Research”?

Mack, McDonnell, Jain, Wang, Froehlich and Findlater, CHI 2021. University of
Washington and Allen Institute for AI. [Author-hosted manuscript](https://www.llwang.net/assets/pdf/2021_mack_a11yreview_chi.pdf),
DOI 10.1145/3411764.3445412. The analyzed subtitle is *A Literature Survey of
Accessibility Papers in CHI and ASSETS from 1994 to 2019*. The [official Honorable Mention
list](https://chi2021.acm.org/information/5208.html) uses a Systematic Review title
variant. Full substantive text read; manuscript pp. 7 and 20 visually checked.
Single-analyst rhetorical coding; title variation is retained, not silently erased.

## Contribution and architecture
The principal contribution is a field-level literature synthesis: research foci,
methods, participation and historical trends. A coded dataset is an explicit
reusable secondary output. This differs from analyzing papers to infer disciplinary
values without synthesizing the field's research landscape, and from contributing
a dataset with only illustrative analyses.

Order: Introduction → Background and Related Work → Method (dataset creation,
analysis) → Results (communities/problems/contributions, methods, participant
roles, historical trends) → Discussion (foci/opportunities, engagement, process
reflections and limitations) → Conclusion. Two time windows have different jobs:
506 papers receive detailed coding for 2010–2019, while 836 support broader counts
and keyword trends for 1994–2019. The abstract, introduction and method consistently
explain this split. A polished summary must not imply every paper received every
analysis or interchange these denominators.

The abstract states field growth, missing broad synthesis, corpus, two analytical
resolutions, selected findings and critical implications. Introduction contextualizes
the field, explains why taking stock matters, acknowledges narrower surveys, asks
questions, previews evidence and lists four outputs. Background provides disability
history and conceptual perspectives before reviewing previous syntheses and critical
reflections. Those perspectives prepare the discussion's interpretation of observed
patterns. Prior reviews are differentiated by population, coverage and analytical
purpose rather than dismissed because their sample was smaller.

## Review method as an argument
The method explains what qualifies as accessibility research, why metadata terms
were used, and why broad aging research was not automatically included. Search
scope is therefore an epistemic decision with consequences, not just a reproducible
string. The authors report false-positive screening, false-negative checks,
different retrieval procedures for different periods, metadata repairs and residual
missingness. They also acknowledge platform search changes that impede exact
reproduction. Screening and coding are separate operations with distinct reviewer
arrangements. The later codebook work combines deductive questions with iterative
inductive refinement and reports where agreement was assessed; it does not imply
all 506 papers were independently double coded.

Results give counts and examples, explicitly distinguish papers and participant
groups, and explain multi-label codes. Historical keyword frequency is recognized
as an imperfect proxy for actual methods. Discussion then moves beyond counting:
whose concerns receive attention, which problem framings may reproduce assumptions,
and what research choices merit reconsideration. The observed median sample size
is a description, not a recommended sample size for new studies. Low frequency
alone does not prove a topic is important; the discussion supplies conceptual and
community-oriented reasons for its priorities. Proposed explanations for attention
imbalances remain hypotheses, not causes established by bibliometrics.

## Micro-analysis A: connect an analytic plan to situated judgment
Manuscript p. 7, §3.2 opening, locator “We qualitatively coded”. Four sentences.
S1 separates the two datasets and analytical resolutions. S2 identifies subjective
judgment and researchers' positions. S3 explains experience differences in the
team. S4 states commitments and their influence on interpretation. Flow: **analysis
scope → judgment provenance → expertise context → epistemic qualification**.
The previous paragraph checks retrieval coverage; the following subsection details
codebook development. Positionality appears as part of how knowledge is produced,
not a replacement for operational reporting.

## Micro-analysis B: derive review guidance from encountered difficulty
Manuscript p. 20, §5.3 opening, locator “Rigorously curating”. Six sentences.
S1 claims that curation/coding effort matters to assessing reviews. S2 gives the
metadata-cleaning difficulty. S3 adds coding-consistency work. S4 derives the need
for sufficient methodological detail. S5 projects increased difficulty with
multiple sources or automation. S6 differentiates agreement expectations across
review approaches. Flow: **methodological claim → two grounded difficulties →
reporting consequence → transfer condition → method-specific qualification**.
It follows substantive research recommendations and precedes terminology and scope
limitations. The final sentence is the authors' methodological stance; a skill
should not turn it into a universal rule requiring one reliability statistic for
all review traditions.

## Transfer and exceptions
For a review manuscript, align question, inclusion boundary, unit of analysis,
extraction, synthesis and claim. Make coverage failures visible. Use results to
establish patterns, then supply a warrant for why those patterns matter. Distinguish
a mapping review from effect-size meta-analysis and a purposive critical synthesis.
A field survey can contribute concepts, critique and a reusable corpus without
pooling treatment effects. Do not copy this two-window design when the research
question has no reason for different temporal resolutions.
