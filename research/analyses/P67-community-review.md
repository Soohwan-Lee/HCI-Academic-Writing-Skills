# P67 — Community-collaborative approaches: synthesis of publication discourse

## Source and reading boundary

Cooper, Horne, Hayes, Heldreth, Lahav, Holbrook, and Wilcox, CHI 2022, *A Systematic Review and Thematic Analysis of Community-Collaborative Approaches to Computing Research*, DOI 10.1145/3491102.3517716. Read the complete main text of the 18-page [arXiv v1 manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.04171v1), including methods, limitations, findings, and discussion; references provide source context, not independently verified project evidence. The first page supplies the conference identity and DOI. This is single-analyst, version-dependent rhetorical coding. Award status is unverified. Selection rests on its explicit distinction between systematic collection and interpretive contribution, and its careful separation of reported practices from actual practices.

## Architecture and contribution

The sequence is Introduction → Background (action research; community-based approaches; participation debates) → Method (collection, analysis, positionality, limitations) → descriptive Findings → thematic Findings organized around establishing partnerships, participatory decision-making, and sustaining results → Discussion (structural changes, methodological proposals, reflexive practice) → Conclusion. Seven dimensions sit within three project phases. This organization combines descriptive mapping with critical interpretation rather than forcing every result into a uniform theme subsection.

The abstract moves from a community-oriented disciplinary shift to missing systematic understanding, the 47-paper review, seven dimensions, a cross-cutting power interpretation, and proposed implications. Introduction pp. 1–2 turns disciplinary silos into a consequential problem: insufficient grounding risks extractive engagement. It defines a deliberately invented umbrella term, states an overarching question and three subsidiary RQs, then distinguishes review, thematic interpretation, and methodological proposals as contributions. Background pp. 2–4 reconstructs the relationship among AR, PD, and CBPR before questioning whether participation is intrinsically beneficial. Prior work supplies evaluative commitments against which the corpus can be interpreted; it is neither a competitor inventory nor merely proof of an empty topic.

## Evidence, warrant, and boundaries

Systematic searching and screening are collection methods; reflexive thematic analysis is explicitly the primary contribution-producing analysis (§3). Five databases and stated search fields, date/language restrictions, eligibility criteria, and a flowchart make the corpus construction inspectable. Screening agreement is reported separately from interpretive analysis. Coding discussion supports richer interpretations and productive disagreement, rather than treating agreement statistics as the validity criterion for generated themes.

The discussion argues that computing publications foreground some participatory stages while leaving problem formulation and analysis more researcher-controlled. The warrant combines corpus examples and descriptive frequencies with commitments articulated in AR/CBPR literature. These support a critique of publication discourse and research structures, not causal proof about every underlying collaboration. Positionality identifies predominantly Western research institutions and researchers' own privileged role. Limitations explicitly warn that unreported community benefits may nevertheless have occurred. Methodological proposals remain proposals; there is no evaluation showing that implementing them solves power imbalances. The conclusion preserves tensions instead of announcing their resolution.

## Two paragraph micro-analyses

Rendered PDF p. 4, §3.1.1, opening **“We did not include gray”**, is a complete six-sentence paragraph. S1 states an exclusion. S2 specifies the excluded community-produced material. S3 acknowledges its substantive importance. S4 justifies the boundary through the paper's target—scholarly representation. S5 locates the authors within broader dissemination practices. S6 links institutional recognition to both the omission and later critique. The preceding paragraph defines search scope; the following specifies database fields and dates. This is decision-first writing, not a conclusion-first empirical argument: concession and justification keep the exclusion visible as an analytic limitation.

Rendered PDF p. 7, §3.4, opening **“In addition, our findings include”**, has four sentences. S1 introduces descriptive statistics; S2 restricts their referent to reported elements; S3 explains possible incompleteness; S4 illustrates the restriction through community/researcher outcomes. The preceding paragraph balances breadth against study-level depth. The next section introduces descriptive findings. Its first sentence is a topic orientation; the interpretive constraint arrives in S2–S4. Placing that constraint immediately before results helps prevent readers from treating absence in papers as absence in projects.

## Transfer conditions and exceptions

Use this architecture when the knowledge object is a field's representation of practice and the analysis interprets commitments, tensions, and omissions. Keep corpus-selection rigor distinct from the warrant for interpretive claims. Place crucial limitations before results when they determine what a frequency means. Do not copy its terminology or epistemological labels as universal method instructions; justify the actual analytic stance. Neither a technical benchmark nor a quantitative meta-analysis should inherit this interpretive architecture merely because it also collects papers.
