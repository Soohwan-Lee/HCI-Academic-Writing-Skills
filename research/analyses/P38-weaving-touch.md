# P38 — Weaving by Touch

**Scope.** Das, Borgos-Rodriguez, and Piper, CHI 2020, *Weaving by Touch: A Case Analysis of Accessible Making*. Author-hosted proceedings PDF, DOI 10.1145/3313831.3376477; Honorable Mention indicated by the ACM publication badge. Full substantive text read; PDF p. 10 checked visually. Single-analyst interpretation, not independent reliability coding.

**Knowledge object and warrant.** This is primarily an interpretive empirical account of accessible making, with a substantive conceptual reframing of accessibility through materiality, interdependence, and labor valuation. Eight months of participant observation, 30 sessions totaling approximately 60 hours, contextual interviews, fieldnotes, and video support the account. The authors observed 19 blind or visually impaired weavers and three sighted instructors in a communal studio. They did not introduce or evaluate the studio as a new system. Their participation as volunteers is the fieldwork relationship; examples of future tangible interfaces are implications, not tested artifacts.

**Actual structure.** Abstract → Introduction → Related Work (disabled making, material practices, ability-diverse collaboration) → Method (context, observation, interviews, analysis/positionality) → Findings (material properties, negotiating co-creation, valuing labor) → Discussion (materiality, empowered co-creation, value of making) → Conclusion. Findings first explain weaving mechanics so readers can understand later tactile evidence. Methods describe researcher sightedness and power, rather than relegating positionality to a generic closing limitation paragraph.

**Opening argument.** The abstract moves from maker opportunity and accessibility's established independence framing to an alternative site, then three analytic dimensions and future tensions. Introduction pp. 1–2 expands this: democratization claims meet exclusion critiques; accepted accessible-making approaches and their benefits are acknowledged; unresolved assumptions concern individual tools, sustaining institutions, and political circumstances. The authors then justify traditional weaving as a demanding, informative case, describe eight months of inquiry, and state three contributions. The gap is an insufficient conceptual account, not simply too few studies of blind weavers. A numbered RQ list is unnecessary to the actual rhetorical sequence.

**Related-work synthesis.** Each subsection gives a distinct warrant for the same study. Disability-making work establishes why situated community-led practice extends workshops and retrospective accounts. Materiality research supplies ways to read texture, tension, and collisions while identifying whose experiences that scholarship has centered. Ability-diverse collaboration gives a relational account of access and positions the case as an extension into creative learning. These are intersecting conversations, not three independent inventories. Their relations return explicitly in the discussion, where the findings challenge both individual independence and unqualified empowerment rhetoric.

**Claim–support–limit.** Detailed sequences of guidance, workspace adjustment, and co-weaving support the claim that accessibility is negotiated. Observed physical actions, participants' explanations, and material behavior are interpreted together; a quotation does not carry the argument alone. Accounts of sales, gifts, recognition, and exclusion support the tension between empowerment and structural disadvantage. The warrant is situated interpretive adequacy, not evidence that this organizational model improves outcomes over alternatives. One assisted-living community, volunteers' partial access, and researcher positionality bound the account. Proposed haptic and acoustic interfaces remain possibilities. The political reading is explicitly an author interpretation, not a unanimous participant theory.

**Paragraph micro-analysis.** Discussion, PDF p. 10, opening locator “Studying this community”: S1 introduces sustaining organizational care work; S2 specifies its range; S3 links sales to sustainability while marking complexity; S4 explains online selling's participatory potential; S5 reverses the benefit through capitalist valuations; S6 questions whether disability framing rather than product quality determines value; S7 offers the history of Outsider Art as an analogical warning; S8 draws a caution for future accessible-making discussion. Relations: topical claim → specification → tension → benefit → counterclaim → question → precedent → normative implication. The opening identifies an analytic object; the paragraph's strongest evaluative conclusion arrives at the end. The preceding paragraph warns against celebrating making apart from oppression; this paragraph extends that warning to organizational valuation, and the conclusion synthesizes material, collaborative, and political findings.

**Reusable move and exception.** Acknowledge a familiar design goal's historical value before showing why the case requires revising it. Match each literature strand to a finding and return to it with evidence. A design-relevant empirical paper can end with unresolved political tensions rather than a list of implementable features. Neither uniformly positive empowerment stories nor mandatory conclusion-first paragraphs describe this example well; paragraph size and section proportion here are observations, not norms.
