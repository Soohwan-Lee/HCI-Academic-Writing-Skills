# P68 — What Is Interaction? A comparative conceptual essay

## Provenance and reading

Hornbæk and Oulasvirta, CHI 2017, DOI 10.1145/3025453.3025765. Read the complete main argument, tables, and conclusion in the 13-page [author-hosted manuscript](https://www.kasperhornbaek.dk/papers/CHI2017_Interaction.pdf). The first-page conference/DOI block and [Aalto publication record](https://research.aalto.fi/fi/publications/what-is-interaction/) establish identity; the author copy is the analyzed version, without claiming publisher-file equivalence. Award status is unverified. This single-analyst reading selects an explicitly bounded conceptual essay for its comparative reasoning and progressive construction of definitions, not its authors' institutional status. The paper's interpretation of individual theories remains an argument to assess, not a neutral consensus taxonomy.

## Actual argumentative architecture

After Introduction, the major sections are Missing in Action: Definitions of Interaction → Concepts of Interaction in HCI's Theories → What Work Do the Concepts Do? → Construing the Human–Computer Relationship → Three Blind Spots in Concepts of Interaction → Understanding Limits of Constructive Power → Interaction, Revisited. There is no separate empirical Method/Results sequence or Related Work compartment. Literature is the argumentative material throughout. Seven perspectives—dialogue, transmission, tool use, optimal behavior, embodiment, experience, and control—are compared through scope, phenomena, values, and design/evaluation support in Table 1.

The abstract identifies a consequential conceptual confusion, promises extraction of existing views, states how their differences matter, and ends with desiderata. Introduction p. 1 establishes the term's ubiquity, juxtaposes that ubiquity with absent or contested definitions, redirects the question from the uniquely correct definition toward usefulness, and explains three benefits. The next section does the diagnostic work that an empirical paper might compress into related work. It distinguishes insufficient explicit definitions from rich concepts embedded within theories: the gap is making assumptions and consequences inspectable, not asserting that earlier scholarship lacks substance.

## Knowledge claim, support, and limits

The contribution is a comparative conceptual account and a normative proposal for stronger concepts. The approach purposively interprets influential theories using Dubin's propositions and Bunge's forms of determination. It does not report systematic search coverage, independent empirical confirmation, or a new artifact evaluation. Selection rationale and acknowledged omissions appear on p. 2; the conclusion requests future systematic reviews. Calling this a systematic literature review would misrepresent its warrant.

Examples make distinctions usable. Fitts' Law supports discussion of narrow, determinate prediction; embodied approaches exemplify broader contextual explanation. The electric-muscle-stimulation example shows how changing conceptual perspectives can generate questions, rather than demonstrating that those questions improve a deployed system. Later distinctions among design-agnostic, design-sensitizing, and design-determining constructs turn the comparison into a critique of the tension between scope and determinacy. Their value depends on accepting the authors' criterion of constructive power; they are not a universal ranking of all HCI knowledge. A writing skill should preserve the distinction between a conceptual warrant and an experimentally validated advantage.

## Two rendered paragraph analyses

PDF p. 2, Concepts section, opening **“Although attempts to define”**, has six sentences. S1 contrasts definitional confusion with the existence of developed embedded concepts. S2 states the extraction task. S3 supplies purposive selection reasons and an exception for control theory. S4 disclaims completeness. S5 names missing perspectives. S6 argues why the selected perspectives nevertheless deserve attention. The preceding subsection identifies three gaps; the following paragraph introduces Table 1 and qualifies its synthesis. This is claim-first followed by method and scope qualification, not six interchangeable topic sentences. The closing justification prevents admitted incompleteness from being mistaken for arbitrary selection.

PDF p. 9, Constructive Power section, opening **“To address this”**, contains nine sentences. S1 establishes a definitional task; S2 offers an intuitive interpretation; S3 makes its expected benefit concrete; S4 draws an intermediate inference; S5 introduces counterfactual reasoning; S6 defines its function; S7 paraphrases its conditional operation; S8 grounds it in Fitts' Law; S9 states the constructed definition of constructive power. The preceding paragraph explains why design support remains unresolved; the next decomposes the definition using the same example. This is a genuine argument-last paragraph: the opening announces the problem, while the precise definition is earned through the intermediate reasoning.

## Transfer and exceptions

For conceptual synthesis, let headings expose argumentative dependencies and return to the title question only after distinctions exist to answer it. State corpus selectivity before comparison and keep illustrative applications distinct from evaluation evidence. Definitions can legitimately arrive at a paragraph's end. Do not impose an early final answer, formal RQ list, exhaustive-review apparatus, or empirical results section when doing so obscures the actual intellectual work.
