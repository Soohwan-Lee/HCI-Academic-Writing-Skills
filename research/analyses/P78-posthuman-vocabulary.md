# P78 — Navigating the Posthuman Turn in Computing and Design

## Source and supplementary role

Çağdaş Dedeoğlu and Priyank Chandra, *Navigating the Posthuman Turn in Computing and Design: A Posthuman Vocabulary*, COMPASS 2025, DOI 10.1145/3715335.3735487. This is a supplementary cross-venue contrast, not a CHI, CSCW, UIST, DIS, or IUI paper. The author-hosted 26-page conference-format PDF identifies Yorkville University/The Posthuman Lab and University of Toronto. Crossref verifies title and publisher pagination 504–529. Award status is unverified. All main text through Conclusion was read; the appendix's corpus-classification structure was inspected without independently recoding its 151 entries. Two complete paragraphs were rendered. This is single-analyst, version-dependent analysis.

The primary knowledge object is an interpretive literature synthesis and conceptual vocabulary. The method is a self-described semi-systematic ACM review with reflexive thematic analysis and genealogical interpretation. Its normative design principles are a subsequent argument, not direct empirical findings about which designs improve sustainability. The supplementary selection adds ecological and critical-synthesis breadth while preserving venue identity.

## Structure and rhetorical progression

Actual sections are Introduction; Background, with a posthuman genealogy and theorizing of design; Methods, covering identification, filtering, reading/tracking, and limitations; Mapping the Posthuman in Computing and Design; Discussion: A Posthuman Design Vocabulary; Conclusion; acknowledgments; references; Appendix A, corpus classifications. Mapping includes general trends, three overlapping phases, scholarly engagement, and technological/ecological concerns. Discussion organizes five principles with a table connecting concepts to problems.

The abstract introduces growing concerns, states the 151-paper corpus, advances a three-phase interpretation, proposes five principles, and describes their intended role. The introduction justifies choosing the term posthuman rather than neighboring terms, states an onto-epistemological commitment, describes scope and outputs, gives two contributions, and then expands the sustainability/power rationale. Thus stakes recur after the contribution statement. A rigid background-to-contribution-only ordering would misdescribe this example.

Related work is primarily Background, not a separately titled Related Work section. Genealogy supplies five sensitizing concepts before corpus interpretation; the design-theorizing subsection positions existing frameworks. Mapping then uses citations as reviewed evidence, and Discussion reinterprets that evidence to propose principles. Those citation roles must remain distinguishable. The same conceptual framework both guides reading and informs the output, so the vocabulary should not be presented as arising from theory-free induction.

## Claims, support, warrants, and limits

The review retrieves 216 papers using explicit posthuman/design terms through September 2024 and filters to 151. It reports exclusions, metadata collection, collaborative reflexive analysis, and emic/etic movement. Counts and chronological patterns support claims about the selected corpus; paper examples support interpretations of engagement and conceptual tendencies. Overlapping ecological and technological categories are based on stated objectives rather than object labels alone. The appendix exposes paper-level classifications for inspection.

The three phases are interpretive and overlapping, not statistically detected discontinuities. The claim that five principles should be integrated relies on political and theoretical commitments plus selected examples, not on frequency alone. Limitations specify one database, full-paper scope, and omitted adjacent vocabularies. Earlier unlabelled contributions may be missed. The text's suggestion that full papers engage more rigorously is an author rationale, not a justified universal quality hierarchy. This version also labels post-humanism as Design-PT in Conclusion although the framework elsewhere uses Design-PH; that apparent label error should not propagate into a skill. Selection does not mean endorsing every rhetorical or conceptual choice.

## Paragraph micro-analysis A

PDF p.5, §3.4 Limitations, locator “This is a semi-systematic”: five sentences, boundaries visually verified. S1 names the review type and distinguishes its objectives/flexibility. S2 argues suitability for fragmented fields. S3 explains the interpretive affordance. S4 connects that affordance to this corpus and aim. S5 derives an indicative rather than comprehensive scope. The preceding methodological text explains concern coding; the following paragraph enumerates concrete retrieval boundaries. The opening identifies a method, while the epistemic constraint is earned at the end. Its broad comparison with systematic reviews remains the authors' position, not a universal definition adopted here.

## Paragraph micro-analysis B

PDF p.14, §5, locator “We argue that posthuman”: four sentences, rendered complete. S1 asserts the five-principle normative requirement. S2 contrasts an existing definition with the authors' proposed expansion. S3 formulates their definition after linking it to corpus and discussion. S4 specifies intended political and ethical purposes. The previous paragraph develops undesigning; the next qualifies the proposal against producing new binaries and neglecting marginalized communities. This is claim-first argumentation, but its warrant is conceptual/normative rather than experimental proof.

## Transferable choices

State the purpose and boundary of a review before interpreting its coverage. Separate corpus observations, theoretical interpretation, and normative proposals. Expose classifications and acknowledge terminology-dependent blind spots. For a critical synthesis, the contribution can be a reasoned vocabulary rather than a list of literature gaps; it still requires an inspectable route from selection through interpretation to claims.
