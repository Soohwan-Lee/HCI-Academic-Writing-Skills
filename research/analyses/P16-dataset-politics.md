# P16 — Do Datasets Have Politics?

Morgan Klaus Scheuerman, Emily Denton, and Alex Hanna. CSCW2 2021, article 317.
[Publication](https://doi.org/10.1145/3476058) · [Analyzed author copy](https://www.morgan-klaus.com/pdfs/pubs/Scheuerman-CSCW2021-datapolitics.pdf).

## Selection and contribution

Selected to add a critical, qualitative analysis of a social/disciplinary phenomenon,
contrasting practitioner interviews and controlled experiments. The object of analysis is
how dataset creators document and value their work. Although its materials are papers,
this is not primarily a literature review synthesizing scientific results, nor a new
computer-vision dataset contribution. Its primary knowledge object is an interpretive
empirical account of disciplinary values. The authors also release research materials;
that release supports scrutiny and reuse but does not, by itself, establish an independent
resource contribution. Best Paper is reported on the [official CSCW awards page](https://cscw.acm.org/2021/awards/)
and corroborated by the [author's conference-blog account](https://medium.com/acm-cscw/do-datasets-have-politics-804f169fbd9b).

## Observed structure and opening

Introduction → related work → methods → findings → discussion → conclusion. The methods
start with researcher positionality, then explain corpus construction, sampling, codebook,
structured and thematic coding, and material access. Findings organize observations at
three levels: disciplinary practices, valued data properties, and human actors. Discussion
reorganizes these observations into four value tensions and accompanying recommendations.
The discussion therefore performs a second level of interpretation; it does not merely
repeat the findings headings with implications appended.

The abstract moves from data's significance to the research focus, corpus and analytic
methods, findings, interpretive tensions, and possible changes in practice. The introduction
(pp. 1–4) establishes societal stakes, explains how datasets structure technical research,
compares documentation proposals and prior critiques, and argues why undocumented values
matter. It then gives three explicit questions, explains the corpus and paired analyses,
previews findings organization, and states the four tensions. This longer explanatory
opening supplies concepts a cross-disciplinary reader needs; it is a counterexample to
forcing every paper into a very short background paragraph.

## Related work as an argument

Section 2 connects values in scientific/design practice, critiques of vision datasets,
and documentation frameworks. Its comparison axes include scope, prospective versus
retrospective orientation, and attention to explicit values. Those axes justify studying
existing documentation across tasks. The authors recognize neighboring contributions
before narrowing the present study's focus. Novelty statements are author claims situated
in 2021, not findings that this writing analysis independently verifies against all work.

## Two paragraph micro-analyses

**Introduction, p. 2, locator “Given the centrality”.** S1 links datasets' central role to
reporting initiatives; S2 gives examples; S3 characterizes their prospective purpose; S4
identifies limited attention to articulating values; S5 narrows the scope of retrospective
critiques; S6 asserts the missing broader examination; S7 states the resulting knowledge
gap; S8 connects that gap to disciplinary reflexivity. The progression is context →
examples → classification → limitation → contrast → gap → consequence. Its opening is
contextual, not the paragraph's final conclusion. The previous paragraph explains what
datasets do technically; the next argues what failure to articulate values makes invisible.
This is a local bridge from the technical object to its social significance.

**Discussion 5.1, p. 25, locator “A counterexample within our”.** S1 introduces a dataset
that prioritizes care; S2 describes parental consent; S3 adds ethics-review requirements;
S4 interprets the slower process as attention to autonomy; S5 identifies further practices
that could strengthen care. Claim/example → evidence → additional evidence → warrant →
qualification. The preceding paragraph synthesizes neglected care; this paragraph prevents
that synthesis from becoming exceptionless. The following subsection translates the value
into recommendations. The example is neither ornamental nor an unqualified success story.
Paragraph boundaries for both examples were checked against rendered pages.

## Evidence and limits

The authors analyze 114 publications associated with 113 datasets, combining structured
observations with thematic reading. Section 3.6 explains why they did not use a formal
inter-rater metric, describing cross-check coding and consistency checks instead. This
supports a method-specific account of rigor, not a universal rule against reliability
metrics. Findings use counts, named cases, excerpts, and counterexamples; discussion
connects them to a declared interpretive standpoint.

A crucial boundary is documentation versus actual practice: an unreported action is not
a proven absent action. Absence and emphasis can support an argument about disciplinary
communication, but claims about personal motives require additional evidence. The corpus
combines highly cited datasets with stratified sampling, covers a particular historical
range, and cannot establish all contemporary ML practice. The paper has no separate
limitations section; positionality and qualifications are distributed. Recommendations
are normative proposals, not experimentally validated improvements.

## Transferable writing decisions

Use a findings-to-discussion change of analytical level when it creates new understanding.
Connect counts and excerpts to an explicit interpretive warrant. Include counterexamples
before recommendations. Preserve the distinction between what documents say, how the
analyst interprets it, and what ought to change. These are single-analyst observations and
editorial transfer judgments; neither an award nor these notes proves a style's efficacy.
