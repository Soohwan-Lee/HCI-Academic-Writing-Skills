## Architecture and opening

Actual sequence: Introduction → Conversations in Craft and Computation (From Computation to Craft; From Craft to Computation) → Craft Histories (Scandinavian Slöyd; British Arts and Crafts; Japanese Mingei) → Discussion (material kinships; head/heart/hand; collaborative pedagogy) → Conclusion. The manuscript numbers the third discussion subsection 4.4, following 4.2; preserve this when locating it rather than silently inventing 4.3. There is no standalone Methods section, interview study, or prototype evaluation.

The abstract states the issue and scholarly resources, identifies three historical sites, names three recurring features, and proposes their relevance to computational making. Introduction acknowledges existing work before complicating both craft and computation as historically variable concepts. It identifies two risks—reducing craft to computational operations and freezing it as timeless tradition—then positions historical inquiry as a resource for three lessons. The contribution is a renewed account of relations, techniques, and pedagogy, not discovery that craft can intersect computing.

The related scholarship is organized in two directions. The first examines how computation draws from materials, tools, communities, and pedagogies of craft. The second examines craft scholars’ responses, including disagreements about digital materiality and embodied knowledge. The closing paragraph turns the underexplored origins of craft into questions for historical analysis. This structure makes reciprocal perspectives visible instead of arranging an author-by-author inventory. Histories then provide substantial cases with primary-source passages and later historical interpretations; discussion returns across cases to shared themes.

## Claims, historical support, and qualifications

Slöyd ties materials and bodily learning to educational formation. Morris’s workshop brings political commitments, sensory dye knowledge, and contemporary chemistry together. Onta pottery shows ecological rhythms and communal skill alongside gendered restrictions and bureaucratic attempts to preserve practice unchanged. The support is documentary/historical reconstruction and interpretation. The warrant for computational provocations is a reasoned comparison of relations and commitments across these histories and contemporary practices. It is not a measured effect of introducing a craft curriculum or a new computational artifact.

The three cases are chosen formative examples, not an exhaustive systematic review or representative sample of global craft. The argument contains speculative bridges explicitly oriented toward what computing might become. These must remain proposals in a derived writing skill. The conclusion acknowledges that the histories are incomplete and presented favorably, then develops concrete counterweights: exclusion, nationalism, privilege, restrictive traditions, elite consumption, and craftwashing. The Onta narrative already anticipates these concerns, so the caveat is grounded in the paper’s own material rather than attached as ritual humility.

## Paragraph micro-analysis

**p. 250:18, Conclusion, “Of course, the stories” (eight sentences; rendered).** S1 limits completeness and the favorable framing. S2 states that not all craft practices should transfer. S3 identifies gender norms, nationalism, and discipline as counterexamples. S4 specifies exclusion through kinship and inherited privilege. S5 adds object fetishization. S6 explains how narrow production stories can exaggerate separation from other material worlds. S7 develops the danger of nostalgic elite consumption, with Morris’s prices as a concrete instance. S8 connects this to commercial craftwashing. Relations: qualification → principle → cumulative counterevidence → social consequence. The preceding paragraph speculates about craft and AI education; the following reasserts bounded virtues for computation. This extended qualifying paragraph is not a short conclusion-first claim plus homogeneous evidence: its accumulating harms constrain the otherwise generative proposal.

## Transfer and exception

Historical contributions can organize the body by cases and the discussion by cross-case concepts. Keep contemporaneous voices, later scholarship, analyst interpretation, and future speculation distinguishable. Preserve difficult material that complicates an appealing analogy. Do not retrofit an empirical study template onto a documentary argument, but do explain source selection and inferential limits when making historical claims. This paper is also not permission to replace a systematic-review protocol with illustrative histories when the intended claim concerns literature prevalence or coverage.
