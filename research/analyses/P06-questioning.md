# P06 — Questioning the AI

Liao, Gruen, and Miller. CHI 2020.
[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/3313831.3376590) · [Analyzed arXiv copy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02478).
The downloaded PDF identifies itself as v3, 3 September 2021, with an updated question-bank
figure. Treat this analysis as version-specific, not an exact transcription of the 2020
camera-ready artifact.

## Contribution and evidence

Primary route: qualitative empirical account of design practice. Secondary route:
reusable design resource. Interviews with 20 practitioners, supported by an
algorithm-informed question bank, reveal gaps between available XAI techniques and
practical explainability needs. The resulting expanded question bank is both informed
by these findings and proposed for future use. Its presence does not make this a
system-performance paper or an effectiveness evaluation of the resource.

## Observed architecture

Introduction → background → question-bank development → study design → results →
discussion, including limitations → conclusion. Headings are unnumbered in the PDF.
The probe has a section before the study because readers need to understand how it
shaped the inquiry. Results have two main organizing layers: general design-practice
themes, then detailed explainability-needs categories. This combines an interpretive
account with a structured comparison against a pre-existing resource.

## Opening moves

The abstract establishes the algorithm-to-user gap, identifies practitioners and
interviews, explains the question bank as probe, and names empirical and resource
contributions. It does not follow an experimental abstract's effect-size pattern.

The introduction (pp. 1–2) moves from increased explainability interest to a concrete
mismatch between an algorithmic explanation and a user's purpose. It recognizes existing
human-centered approaches before proposing a complementary practitioner perspective.
It explains why designers occupy a bridging role, why the early state of practice
requires a probe, and how questions can connect user needs with technical capabilities.
Three contributions conclude the introduction. No standalone numbered RQ list is required
for this inquiry to have a clear purpose.

## Paragraph and sentence flow

Introduction, p. 1, opening locator **“Recently, an increasing number”**: S1 establishes
the availability of XAI toolkits; S2 contrasts availability with limited knowledge of
practical use; S3 names the challenge of connecting needs and capabilities; S4 identifies
designers as the people doing that connecting work; S5 derives the decision to study
them. The paragraph moves context → contrast/gap → problem → actor/warrant → study
focus. It is not conclusion-first: its main methodological consequence arrives last.
The preceding paragraph offers a complementary practitioner perspective; this paragraph
justifies that perspective, while the next explains why the inquiry needs a probe.
The progression narrows from a research community to a practice problem, then to the
means of investigating it.

## Related-work positioning

Background (pp. 2–3) has three strands: XAI, support for AI practitioners, and
question-driven explanation. Each establishes a different premise of the design:
explanations depend on recipients; designers need support connecting needs and
capabilities; questions can represent explanation needs. The section acknowledges nearby
guidelines and design processes, then narrows their relationship to the present inquiry.
The question-bank section makes an especially useful distinction: a taxonomy of model
properties is not necessarily a taxonomy of what users need to understand.

## Claim, warrant, and boundary

Study design and analysis (pp. 4–5) describe recruitment within IBM, interview activities,
iterative coding, and a separate comparison of elicited questions with the initial bank.
Findings (pp. 5–9) connect interpretive statements to contextualized participant examples
and identify both covered and uncovered needs. Discussion turns these observations into
candidate resources and research directions. Those directions remain proposals.

Limitations (p. 10) explicitly separate practitioner accounts from direct end-user
needs, restrict organizational and product coverage, and reject exhaustive coverage of
XAI techniques. The paper also describes variability factors as sensitizing concepts,
not a predictive model. A transferable skill should retain these distinctions rather
than turn illustrative needs into universal requirements.

## Writing moves worth transferring

- Explain the participant group's epistemic position: what they can tell us and what
  they cannot directly establish.
- Justify a probe before describing how it was administered.
- Alternate interpretation, situated evidence, and consequence within findings.
- Separate the initial analytic scaffold from additions generated through inquiry.
- Link recommendations to findings without claiming their effectiveness was tested.
