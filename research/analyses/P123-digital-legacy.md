## Contribution contract and review subtype
A Grounded Theory Literature Review synthesizes four thematic interests and interprets their relationships through a digital legacy data lifecycle: encoding, accessing and dispossessing. The central gain is the move from separate topics to multi-user and multi-generational relationships. The lifecycle is a conceptual synthesis of literature, not a longitudinal observation of every legacy or an estimated causal process.

Two searches yielded 24 and seven additional papers, 31 total. The first ACM/SIGCHI search returned 3,367 records, reduced to 1,832 after cleaning, then 24 after relevance screening. The second widened to the top 20 HCI venues by the stated ranking. It reports seven additions but not a complete second search denominator. English-language, end-of-life digital legacy boundaries exclude neighboring literatures. Workshop research papers can be included while proposals and panels are excluded. Systematic selection and interpretive synthesis coexist; the paper does not claim meta-analysis or a PRISMA protocol.

## Architecture
Introduction → methods and two rounds of selection/analysis → definitions and four thematic findings → lifecycle interpretation, dependencies, multi-user and multi-generational considerations → challenges and CSCW's role → limitations → conclusion. The four themes are not the three lifecycle stages. Treating them as interchangeable would erase the conceptual work performed in discussion.

The abstract starts from end-of-life support and accumulated scholarship, calls for stock-taking, identifies the synthesis method and themes, presents the model, then explains its use. It prioritizes the conceptual result over corpus size. The introduction first makes inheritance humanly consequential, then shows its social/temporal complexity. Contrasting prior emphases establish fragmentation; a critical mass of work earns synthesis. The final moves preview the themes, relationship-based model and CSCW-facing contribution.

## Related work and conceptual lineage
A separate related-work section would duplicate the analyzed literature. Background literature appears in the introduction; corpus papers supply findings; information lifecycle scholarship supplies a discussion resource. The authors acknowledge the resemblance to acquisition/possession/dispossession/reacquisition, then explain their departures toward encoding and access. This sequence credits inheritance and makes the extension assessable.

Exclusion examples do analytical work: technical memory, legacy computing systems and end-of-life care without digital inheritance are not the same research object. These examples clarify a boundary that keywords alone cannot settle. They do not prove exhaustive coverage of all adjacent fields.

## Evidence and warrant
Open, axial and selective coding, memos and team discussion support the synthesis. Papers are read at each coding stage by at least two team members. The seven later papers are analyzed separately before comparison; the authors acknowledge residual confirmation bias. This supports an additional interpretive check, not independent external validation. An inter-rater statistic is not automatically required for this analysis tradition.

The findings attribute interests and claims to scholarship. Discussion interprets relationships, identifies what the model makes visible, and proposes future work. Distinguish reported participant experiences in primary papers from this review's secondary interpretation and its normative research agenda. Western and HCI-focused source boundaries remain consequential.

## Model-to-agenda flow
The discussion first explains stages, then dependencies, then actors and generations. Figures progressively add these dimensions rather than repeating a single diagram. The challenges section converts this framework into questions about death circumstances, abandonment, ownership, crafted versus potential legacies, deployments, accessibility and cultural practice. Some questions follow specific transitions; others arise from corpus-wide omissions. Do not claim every recommendation was mechanically deduced from the model.

## Root-reviewed tensions and reuse limits
- The first search summary and body differ on the second query date: November 2 versus November 3, 2022. Preserve the discrepancy in provenance.
- Search roots appear as both memor* and mem*; no independent query replay was performed.
- Claims that literature ignores abandonment are stronger than the paper's own discussions of forgotten and burner accounts. A precise gap needs a missing relationship or treatment, not absolute absence.
- An introductory endpoint of final deletion sits uneasily with the later repeated lifecycle and dispossession without deletion. Do not impose a terminal trajectory on all cases.
- The model depicts a reading of scholarship. Its arrows do not show measured causal effects or observed transitions for every person.
- The additional seven-paper analysis is not an independently replicated model. Claude flagged a table/corpus citation-count discrepancy, but without full corpus reconstruction this remains unverified and is not adopted as a finding.
- CSCW's proposed suitability is a reasoned disciplinary invitation, not a comparative effectiveness result.

Useful transfer: distinguish mapping from conceptual synthesis, show exactly how categories become relationships, credit the model's ancestry, and return each proposed gap to a bounded corpus or a stated normative premise.
