# P07 — Re-examining human–AI design difficulty

Yang, Steinfeld, Rosé and Zimmerman. CHI 2020.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3313831.3376301
Full-text location and checksum: ../corpus.json. Single-analyst interpretation
of the inspected 12-page copy; PDF page numbers below. Award status is recorded in the verification update below.

## Contribution and evidence
Author claims: synthesis of design challenges, provocative questions that reframe
those challenges, and a framework structuring their underlying design complexity
(Introduction, pp. 1–2). Our primary label: conceptual framework; synthesis of
prior studies, design experience and teaching is its method/warrant. The paper
explicitly bounds its nonrepresentative, largely self-derived case material in
Method (p. 5). It is not a controlled evaluation proving a framework improves UX.
CMU affiliation is stated on p. 1; prestige does not validate any rhetorical device.

## Architecture and moves
Actual sequence: Introduction → Related Work → Three Questions for Provocation
→ Method → The Framework → Using the Framework → Conclusion and Acknowledgement.
The abstract starts with opportunities and problems, questions the distinctiveness
of those problems, identifies a synthesis approach, names two dimensions and four
levels, and states usefulness. The introduction proceeds from AI opportunities to
designer difficulty, challenges familiar explanations by comparison with other
technologies, states the goal, previews its four-part argument and contributions.
It has interrogative motivation, but no required numbered RQ/hypothesis block.

Related Work (pp. 2–4) maps existing challenges onto design-process stages and
organizes attempted remedies into five themes. The maps do argumentative work:
fragmented explanations motivate the next section's questions. Its function is
not merely to establish that the authors have read the field. The transition from
literature to provocation to method is a deliberate alternative to one final
sentence asserting that prior work is scarce.

The Framework (pp. 6–8) defines dimensions before combining them; Using the
Framework (pp. 8–10) works through contrasting system classes and identifies
implications for different audiences. Worked applications illustrate analytical
utility; they do not establish comparative design effectiveness. Reuse that
claim/evidence distinction when writing a conceptual paper.

## Paragraph micro-analysis
Introduction, p. 1, third prose paragraph, opening locator: “Existing research
frequently attributes”. Six sentences, visually checked against original layout.
- S1 identifies prevailing explanations, with citations: establishes the target.
- S2 contrasts them with HCI's ability to handle comparable complexity: tests
  whether those explanations are sufficient rather than merely listing a gap.
- S3 turns the contrast into a focused question about prototyping.
- S4 adds a parallel counterargument about designing dynamic interactions.
- S5 poses the corresponding question about sketching.
- S6 states the inferred need to articulate what is distinctive about AI design.

The main inference is at the end: a contrast-led, inductive paragraph, not a
conclusion-first paragraph. Its predecessor establishes concrete design trouble;
its successor turns the inferred need into the paper's explicit goal and roadmap.
The repeated objects (design difficulty, prototyping, sketching) make the links
legible without relying on a generic transition at every sentence.

## Transfer and limits
Use a competing-explanation paragraph when the contribution reframes a problem.
Show why an explanation is incomplete before proposing dimensions. Introduce
constructs before examples that apply them. State whose practice a framework
informs. Do not copy claims about the capabilities of 2020 AI into a current paper;
this analysis concerns argument structure, not present-day technical facts.
