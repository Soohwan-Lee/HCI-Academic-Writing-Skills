# P32 — Sociocultural Dimensions of Tracking Health and Taking Care

**Source and scope.** Karthik S. Bhat and Neha Kumar, CSCW2 2020, article 129, DOI 10.1145/3415200. Analyzed the 24-page proceedings PDF hosted on a CMU accessibility reading-list archive; substantive body pp. 1–18. The official [CSCW 2020 awards](https://cscw.acm.org/2020/awards/) lists Best Paper. Selection adds cardiac care, family relationships, gender, and urban India. One analyst codes this version; reputation is a discovery criterion, not proof of writing effectiveness.

**Contribution and evidence.** The authors explicitly offer evidence of sociocultural situatedness and a complication of patient empowerment understood as information provision (§1). These are connected interpretive empirical contributions. Interviews with 18 cardiac patients and family members, plus eight doctors, supply the method and support. The study does not contribute a tracking application, clinical treatment, validated empowerment instrument, or causal estimate of tracking benefits. Its interpretive force comes from explaining why collecting or receiving more information may conflict with patients' preferred care arrangements.

**Actual architecture.** Introduction → Related Work → Methodology (collection, analysis, limitations) → Findings (technology use; attitudes to information; patient–doctor interactions; cultural situatedness/collaborative care) → Discussion (situated tracking; empowerment) → Conclusion. Limitations appear before findings, making the sample boundary available while readers interpret evidence. The abstract moves from a growing research area and intervention opportunity to a limitation of the individual-centered framing, study purpose/location, empirical topics, and implications. It does not promise a numerical improvement. The introduction (pp. 1–2) identifies an assumption behind existing work, develops the metaphor of untracked negative spaces, gives the study and setting rationale, then states two contributions. It acknowledges collective-tracking research before narrowing the extension to data generation, avoiding a false claim that social tracking has never been studied.

**Related work as a connected argument.** Section 2 progresses from self-tracking/datafication to collaborative chronic care and then patient empowerment. Each supplies a necessary step: individual data are context-dependent; caregiving distributes work across people; empowerment need not mean maximal information or individual choice. The authors align with ecological and collaborative accounts, then identify what the cardiac-care case changes. Literature returns in discussion to interpret the findings, rather than being exhausted in a citation inventory before method.

**Claim–support–warrant–limit.** Thematic analysis connects tracking enthusiasm and non-use, selective information avoidance, trusted delegation to clinicians, family caregiving, and gendered disclosure. Participant quotations establish the reasoning and tensions behind practices. For example, avoiding stressful information can be purposeful agency rather than deficient literacy, and caregiving can become surveillance. The warrant for rethinking empowerment is that control over engagement includes decisions to delegate or decline information. Proposed shared data streams and stakeholder-inclusive design are implications; no deployed system demonstrates them. Section 3.3 explicitly limits transfer from middle-income participants and notes gender imbalance. The discussion also rejects treating all Global South settings as interchangeable. Doctors' explanations of gender patterns remain accounts within the study, not verified epidemiological facts.

**Paragraph micro-analysis.** Visually verified p. 4, §2.3, opening locator “A common thread”: five sentences. S1 synthesizes the preceding studies into an information-centered account of empowerment. S2 introduces a contrast: alternative forms are possible. S3 gives a cited companionship reframing. S4 adds the logic-of-care alternative to individual information/choice. S5 positions this study through patients' agency over whether to engage. Relations are synthesis → contrast → alternative evidence → conceptual extension → local positioning. The opening is deductive about the literature, but does not state the paragraph's final study contribution. The previous paragraph supplies the studies being synthesized; the following Methodology section explains how the proposed empirical extension was investigated. This is a literature-to-method bridge with the gap earned by comparison.

**Reusable move and exception.** Show how an accepted intervention goal relies on an assumption, then use discrepant practices to revise that assumption. Keep descriptive findings and normative design implications visibly connected without claiming an implication was tested. Do not import the preference for delegation into another population as a cultural rule; that would erase variation and the study's stated limits. Neither this sample nor its award supports universal section proportions or fixed paragraph lengths.
