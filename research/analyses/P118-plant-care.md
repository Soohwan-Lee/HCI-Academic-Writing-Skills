## Contribution contract
The paper promises (a) background knowledge about domestic plant-care cultures; (b) a challenge to short-term, individual-targeting HPI theories of change; and (c) social and family cultures as entanglement nodes for more-than-human HCI. It delivers these through a domestic plant care ecology model, a five-culture typology, and design considerations.

**Method** is separate: a probe-supported ethnographic study with interviews, analysed through matrices and cross-case comparison. **Form** is also separate: a case-typed findings narrative with conceptual diagrams. The terrarium is an instrument, not the contributed artifact. No resulting design is evaluated, so the considerations remain proposals.

## Abstract and introduction moves
The six-sentence abstract runs: trend; conflicting assumptions; method; mechanism finding; typology; design reorientation. The six-paragraph introduction runs: field trajectory from auxiliary interface toward living being; rival assumptions (spontaneous care versus plant blindness); gap; study; findings preview; lettered contributions. The engine is that a literature dispute makes descriptive work consequential, because each assumption implies a different design lever.

## Related-work jobs
§2.1 establishes houseplants as everyday yet underexamined, sets out competing framings (decoration, wellbeing, care), and ends on an unexplained value-action gap. §2.2 sorts prior reviews by object: they characterise researchers' conceptions rather than participants'. It then concedes that noticing studies are useful but do not explain why noticing differs between people. The 'In sum' paragraph partitions the gap into outside and inside HPI, then converts it into the aim. Mauss and Gell appear only in the Discussion, so that theory interprets results rather than framing data collection.

## Evidence, warrant, limits
**Evidence:** 436 recordings, sensor readings, daily photos, a time-lapse, interviews (51,760 transcript words), field notes, and memos. Eleven households hosted probes. P10 and P13 were interview-only, so the interconnectedness culture has probe data from only H5 and H7.

**Warrant:** recorded behaviour is compared with accounts, and cases are compared across a matrix. This supports claims about what occurred around the terrarium and how participants explained it.

**Limits:**
- Childhood transmission rests on retrospective accounts, not direct observation.
- Two weeks cannot observe long-term enculturation.
- The moss was new, instrumented, and accompanied by guidance, so extension to ordinary houseplants is inferential.
- 'Correlate', 'shape', and 'led to' are qualitative pattern claims; no statistical association or causal identification is reported.
- The authors report reactivity, detection gaps, sensor displacement, and an educated, middle-class skew.
- Their claim that social findings largely escaped desirability bias is an argument, not a test.

## Findings-to-discussion transitions
The Findings preamble defines concern and states the model first, so each culture reads as an instantiation. Section 5 restates results as a spectrum, then distinguishes the paper from multi-species sociality work by foregrounding human-human relations. It then changes register: §5.1 moves from an anthropological premise to what design can do, §5.2 from technology-directed curiosity to design priorities, and §5.3 from a two-week pattern to theories of change. The chain is inferential: observed household differences become a proposed lever. Hedges such as 'can' and 'potentially' carry real weight, because no intervention tests that lever.

## Micro-analysis 1: p. 13, §4.1, paragraph opening 'P4 neither shared' (4 sentences)
1. **Baseline:** states that care was not shared with partner or child, embedding the partner's usual role as an aside.
2. **Contrast:** 'However' introduces recordings of the child's activity, unknown to the parents.
3. **Participant uptake:** reports P4's surprise and later acknowledgement of a missed opportunity.
4. **Case verdict:** 'Overall' condenses the case into coded constituents and a causal outcome, pointing to Fig. 2.

The paragraph shows data sources correcting each other. Sentence 4, however, goes beyond sentence 2: a child engaging without parental awareness complicates simple cultural determination, and the paragraph does not exclude other proximate causes of wilting.

## Micro-analysis 2: p. 21, §5.4, first paragraph (7 sentences)
1. **Threat:** names social desirability and participant roles.
2. **Instance:** a participant's concern about unrecorded interaction, read as possible camera effect.
3. **Instance:** late-study increases in three households, hedged with 'perhaps'.
4. **Instance:** accounts exceeding recordings, interpreted as limited camera consciousness.
5. **Mitigation:** camera-awareness questions and data comparison.
6. **Evaluation:** credits triangulation with greater nuance.
7. **Scope defence:** argues the central social claim is largely insulated.

The sequence is an efficient threat-to-validity template. Its weakness lies in sentences 4 and 7. Over-reporting could equally reflect self-presentation or undetected interaction, and social prompts were added to the interviews during the study, so the insulation claim needs more support.

## Cross-paragraph connections
The P4 paragraph (p. 13) enacts the triangulation that p. 21 later claims. H1's unsupported curiosity and H3's parent-enabled exploration form a contrast the Discussion generalises as guidance. Fig. 7's caption supplies a concrete warrant for mixed data: similar humidity signals in H1 and H7 had different causes. The aim on p. 4 is answered by the Findings opening on p. 10 and compressed in §6. Yet P6's gardening parents and H3's self-described inexperienced parents complicate any linear transmission story, showing that the five constituents are not uniformly required.

## Transferable writing lessons and counterexamples
- **Turn a dispute into a gap.** Show that competing assumptions lead to different designs. *Counterexample:* do not claim the dispute is resolved when the data are a small, purposive sample.
- **Sort prior work by object of study.** 'They studied researchers' conceptions' is sharper than 'few studies exist'. *Counterexample:* this fails if the partition merely relabels neighbouring work.
- **Let one data stream unsettle another** within a paragraph. *Counterexample:* do not end with stronger causality than the juxtaposition earned.
- **Structure typologies by a repeated logic:** epigraph, definition, constituents, cases, practical consequence. *Counterexample:* uniformity can hide cases that do not fit, such as Fig. 6's engagement groups cutting across the cultures.
- **Name method hybrids only if each label does work.** 'Co-ethnographer' and 'minimal intervention' justify design choices. *Counterexample:* 'minimal' is strained when §5.2 reports the technology itself attracted curiosity.
- **State positionality with analytic consequences.** The third author's distance is linked to possible insensitivity. *Counterexample:* biography without analytic consequence is decoration.
- **Limit discussion claims to observed scope.** Move from 'in these households' to 'design could explore'. *Counterexample:* §6's generalising 'we commonly learn' reads as a universal claim beyond the sample.

## Root integration review
Root independently verified OSF version metadata through its public API: v1 published 19 March 2025, primary file 67db013e6949579d5ce48910. The official DIS 2025 award is associated with the published paper; this analysis is of its earlier 24-page preprint, not a claim that the two versions match. The proceedings copy was inaccessible. Figure 5 visually contrasts levels across five cultures, while Figure 7 documents why sensor traces alone do not establish quality of care: H1 flooding and H7 sensor burial give different explanations for high humidity. Root checked both complete micro-analysis paragraphs, four and seven sentences respectively.

A qualitative explanatory model can be a legitimate contribution without a randomized experiment. The limitation here is the strength and reach of the causal account, not a blanket prohibition on qualitative explanation. Describe within-case and cross-case warrants, rival explanations, two-week exposure and retrospective childhood accounts. The typology is situated rather than a population partition or universal developmental ladder. The instrumented terrarium is primarily an access method; its use does not make this an artifact-first paper. Figure 5 and the text support consistent maintenance for some ornament cases, so do not generalize that solitary care invariably reduces consistency. Supplementary data were not obtained.
