# P90 — Deep Learning Uncertainty in Machine Teaching

Single-analyst coding of the HAL author manuscript submitted 18 February 2022, downloaded from an author’s site. The file has a repository cover plus 26 manuscript pages; printed page numbers are one less than PDF positions. All substantive sections and appendices were read. Both complete micro-paragraphs were rendered and checked. The contribution is empirical and methodological understanding of uncertainty in interactive teaching, not merely the experimental application.

## Structure and opening argument

The sequence is Abstract → Introduction → Background and Related Work → Benchmark Study → Experimental Study → Results → Discussion → Conclusion → Acknowledgments → References → Appendices A–B. The computational benchmark supplies a choice for the subsequent user study. It has its own results; the later Results section concerns human behavior. This dependency is more informative than putting every method together and every finding afterward.

The abstract (printed p. 1) begins with confident errors, contrasts conventional offline estimation with interactive use, names two uncertainty types, introduces the teaching experiment, and reports mixed outcomes and concrete situations where users notice differences. It ends by questioning whether the distinction is needed. Introduction (pp. 1–2) starts with the human teacher, narrows to nonexpert interpretation, then explains ambiguity and novelty through examples. It contrasts controlled technical datasets with interactive meaning, states three RQs, and previews benchmark, experiment, and findings. The opening assumption is investigable rather than guaranteed: theoretically informative feedback may not improve teaching.

Background (pp. 3–5) separates estimation techniques from novice machine teaching. The first explains what technical measures mean, their computational costs, and why transfer learning complicates their use. The second establishes existing evidence about novices’ curricula and guidance. Together they motivate two linked gaps: selecting a computationally feasible uncertainty estimate and understanding how people use it. Citations therefore support both apparatus validity and the human research question.

## Evidence, warrants, and limits

A benchmark compares embeddings and uncertainty estimators on digit and playing-card datasets; it selects methods for real-time interaction. The card uncertainty labels are explicitly subjective and task-oriented, not a validated benchmark ground truth. Sixteen novices then train a three-class card recognizer in a counterbalanced within-participant comparison. Think-aloud comments, interviews, logged examples, uncertainty estimates, prediction tests, and questionnaires address different aspects of understanding.

No significant condition effect on prediction tests is evidence against the anticipated simple advantage in this task, not an equivalence result. Training-set size and variability correlate with several outcomes; observed curricula were not randomized interventions. Some discussion language makes systematic teaching sound more decisively superior than the direct comparison in §5.2 supports. A reusable writing skill should preserve the distinction between association, self-reported understanding, and experimentally established benefit. Edge-case observations support a more specific contribution: users notice differences in repeated configurations, competing cards, and unrelated objects. Suggested medical uses are future extrapolations from a card task, not clinical evidence. The official IUI 2022 list identifies this paper as Best Paper; that recognition does not enlarge the evidential scope.

## Transferable moves

Show how a technical benchmark makes a human experiment possible. Keep a negative or mixed result in the abstract when it changes the interpretation of the premise. Use observed situations to explain when a distinction matters instead of seeking one overall winner. Reconnect background concepts to annotation problems discovered during research. Avoid turning naturally occurring teaching strategies into causal prescriptions without additional evidence.
