## Architecture and rhetorical progression

Actual sequence: Introduction → Reintroducing Feminism (history; epistemologies) → Feminism in Cognate Fields (STS; product/industrial design; architecture/urban planning; game design) → Feminism in HCI: The State of the Art → Opportunities to Draw on Feminism in HCI Research → Qualities of Feminist Interaction (pluralism; participation; advocacy; ecology; embodiment; self-disclosure) → Conclusion. Definitions and intellectual lineage precede the proposed qualities; examples accompany their exposition.

The abstract opens with an affirmative affinity between feminism and interaction design, explains the stocktaking and integration purpose, moves through adjacent disciplines and HCI examples, and ends with the proposed qualities’ use in design and evaluation. The introduction begins with feminism’s prominence and existing positive effects before identifying HCI’s limited explicit engagement. Increasingly pervasive interaction raises the stakes. The author then narrows from computing broadly to interaction design and differentiates the proposed agenda’s possible outputs. The introductory bullet list forecasts contributions an agenda could support; it should not be rewritten as five empirically established results of this paper.

The literature sequence is constructive: explain a frequently misunderstood tradition, demonstrate its use in neighboring practices, recognize existing HCI achievements, and identify opportunities for more explicit integration. The novelty claim depends on making an already emerging agenda recognizable and generative, not pretending prior feminist design work did not exist. Cross-disciplinary examples end with possible HCI applications, creating a repeated transfer mechanism. The qualities section acknowledges that its individual components are not unique to feminism; their constellation is the proposal.

## Claim, support, and limits

The main claim is that feminist commitments can inform design processes and artifacts, including generative work, rather than only critique finished systems. Support comprises feminist theory, interpretations of prior designs, historical examples, and situated personal experience. The warrant links qualities to concrete design choices and consequences: the Hoosier cabinet illustrates how local efficiency can coexist with a broader gendered burden, while recommender controls illustrate user access to the identities software assigns. These interpretations ground the proposal but do not establish universal causal effects of using the six qualities.

The paper offers neither a controlled evaluation of the qualities nor a replicable corpus selection protocol. The first-person account of unwanted recommendations supplies a specific interpretive case; it is not a representative study. The conclusion explicitly presents the qualities as a starting conversation. A derivative skill should also preserve the historically situated character of the 2010 feminism overview instead of using its wave schema or gender formulations as an exhaustive contemporary taxonomy. Methodological recommendations here concern ethical and epistemological commitments, not a standardized coding procedure.

## Paragraph micro-analysis

**p. 1307, Ecology, “The design rationale behind” (four sentences; rendered).** S1 explains the cabinet’s intended efficiency benefit in its changing labor context. S2 acknowledges success on that criterion. S3 introduces a contrasting ecological consequence and supports it with an advertisement’s framing. S4 condenses the shift in women’s position as the interpretation. Relations: intended purpose → concession → countervailing evidence → concluding claim. The preceding paragraph describes the cabinet and historical circumstances; the following extends the concern to HCI’s ecological perspectives. This paragraph is argument-last: the opening supplies the rationale that the subsequent interpretation complicates. A compulsory conclusion-first rewrite would flatten the productive contrast.

## Transfer and exception

When introducing a conceptual agenda, distinguish inherited concepts, existing examples, your synthesis, and its intended uses. Show what a quality changes in a design reading rather than merely listing values. Acknowledging partial overlap strengthens the novelty boundary. The same architecture is unsuitable for a paper claiming measured intervention effectiveness: examples and normative coherence would then need complementary outcome evidence. It is also not a substitute for a systematic or scoping review protocol when corpus coverage is itself the claim.
