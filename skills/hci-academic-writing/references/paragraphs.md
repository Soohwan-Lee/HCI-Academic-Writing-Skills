# Paragraph and sentence flow

Revise a paragraph by its intellectual job and its connection to neighbors. Smooth
wording cannot repair a missing premise. Before rewriting, state the paragraph's
main question or claim in plain language, identify its support, and mark what it
adds beyond the preceding paragraph. Keep the author's intended epistemic stance.

## Inspect three scales

**Between paragraphs:** identify what the first paragraph establishes and what the
next now needs to answer. Common relations are problem→response, claim→evidence,
comparison→consequence, observation→interpretation, definition→application, or
finding→boundary. A transition should name the actual dependency. “Furthermore”
can announce addition but cannot explain why these two ideas belong together.

**Within a paragraph:** annotate sentence roles briefly when diagnosing a difficult
passage: claim, context, evidence, contrast, explanation, qualification, consequence,
or bridge. Consecutive sentences may share a role when they develop one line of
reasoning. If the paragraph switches to a different reader question, split or move
it. If it contains only a claim and a list, check whether the relation between the
items needs synthesis.

**Within a sentence:** put a recognizable subject near its verb. Start from an
entity or concept the reader can identify, then add the new information needed by
the next sentence. Keep parallel comparisons grammatically parallel. Place a
qualification beside the claim it limits, especially for population, measure,
causality or uncertainty. Avoid replacing a precise repeated term with a synonym
that makes the reader wonder whether the construct changed.

## Sentence-level operations

- **Given to new:** connect the opening to something just established, then place
  the important addition where readers can carry it into the next sentence. Do not
  mechanically move every new term to the end when a definition needs it first.
- **Stable subjects:** use a small, meaningful set of subjects across the paragraph.
  A change from participants to the interface to the researchers should correspond
  to a real change of actor or analytical level, not accidental sentence variety.
- **Make agency explicit:** unpack nominalizations when the actor matters. “The
  exclusion of responses occurred” can become “The analysts excluded incomplete
  responses” when that is what the method actually reports. Do not invent an actor
  or causal relation to obtain a more active sentence.
- **Calibrate verbs:** use “participants reported” for reports, “we observed” for
  observations, “we interpret” for interpretive claims, and “we propose/could” for
  untested designs. An experiment's causal language depends on its design and
  assumptions, not merely a significant test. A nonsignificant comparison is not
  equivalence. Keep estimates and uncertainty where they determine interpretation.
- **Integrate citations:** name a source in the sentence when its particular
  contribution or disagreement matters; use parenthetical support when the claim
  is the focus. Keep each citation attached to the proposition it supports.
- **Use tense purposefully:** past tense usually reports performed procedures and
  encounters; present tense describes the paper's argument, definitions and figures.
  Follow coherent disciplinary and author choices rather than enforcing one tense.
- **Preserve voice:** repair ambiguity and grammar before replacing idiom. Keep
  first-person, embodied or stance-bearing expressions when they carry knowledge.

A local cohesion check can trace a concept through three sentences: “The interface
exposes each member's rationale. These rationales make disagreement visible before
the vote. That visibility may change the interpersonal cost of dissent.” The first
two sentences describe a hypothetical design; the third marks an untested possibility.
The repeated referents connect the reasoning without pretending a social effect
has already been measured.

## Choose a productive local architecture

| Job | Possible progression | When it helps |
|---|---|---|
| Report an established finding | Main pattern → evidence → variation → bounded answer | Reader already understands constructs and comparison |
| Justify a methodological choice | Purpose → alternatives/constraint → choice → consequence | The choice would otherwise appear arbitrary |
| Explain a mechanism | Observation → plausible account → supporting relation → alternative/limit | Interpretation must remain distinguishable from observation |
| Synthesize literature | Shared premise → contrasting cases → implication for the question | Individual source summaries do not reveal the relevant tension |
| Develop a critical reading | Case/context → concession → complicating evidence → interpretation | The conclusion is more intelligible after the case |
| Make a limitation useful | Design choice → missing evidence → consequence → bounded next step | A generic “small sample” sentence does not explain inferential scope |
| Advance a design narrative | Encounter → response in making → reflection → transferable orientation | Situated choices, not only final artifacts, carry knowledge |

These are roles, not five-sentence templates. Use a topic sentence when it lets
readers orient efficiently. A paragraph may instead start with a purpose,
qualification, concrete episode or puzzle and earn its main interpretation later.
Move the conclusion forward only if the reader can understand it without the
missing setup. Preserve meaningful narrative or interpretive voice.

## Source-grounded contrasts

- [P63, Phishing Warnings](https://doi.org/10.1145/1357054.1357219), PDF p.7, follows
  a six-sentence episode from repeated dismissal to protection despite failed
  comprehension, then derives an implication. Argument-last ordering makes the
  unexpected interpretation intelligible; the recommendation remains untested.
- [P49, Feminist HCI](https://doi.org/10.1145/1753326.1753521), p.1307, explains a
  cabinet's efficiency rationale, acknowledges its success, then introduces a
  countervailing social consequence and interpretation. Front-loading an abstract
  moral would remove the concession that the argument depends on.
- [P64, Typing](https://doi.org/10.1145/3173574.3174220), PDF p.8, states a clustering
  purpose, defines the operation, identifies absent ground truth, and explains
  available separation and interpretation criteria. The qualification belongs
  inside the methodological warrant, not only at the paper's end.
- [P62, Fitts' Law Review](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713623), PDF p.5, resolves
  inconsistent terminology through a merge rule, converse exclusion rule, and
  examples. The paired conceptual distinction supplies cohesion without repeated
  transition phrases.
- [P53, Recollecting Craft](https://doi.org/10.1145/3610041), p.250:18, accumulates
  historically specific counterweights to an attractive design analogy. An
  eight-sentence qualifying paragraph is useful here; sentence count alone is no
  reason to split it into disconnected disclaimers.

These examples are selected analytical observations, not measured evidence that a
particular structure improves reader comprehension.

### Three additional relations to consider

- **Inheritance → difference → warrant for transfer → scope.** P119 Social
  Transparency, arXiv v1 p.7, closes related work through four argumentative relations that
  explain why prior human-human concepts are useful for AI-mediated decisions.
  Use this when “unlike prior work” is asserted without explaining what is retained
  and why the changed context matters. A conceptual extension still needs credit
  for its antecedents.
- **Account → contrasting record → uptake → interpretation.** P118 Plant Care,
  OSF v1 p.13, places a caregiver's account beside recorded child interaction and
  the caregiver's uptake of that interaction. Keep each source identifiable; neither
  is ground truth by default. Different moments, meanings or vantage points may
  explain a discrepancy. Bound the interpretation to what the contrast supports.
- **Research presence → concrete signs → effects on material → supported account.**
  P118 p.21 describes camera reactivity. Where a manuscript treats reactivity as a
  validity threat, explain what mitigation shows and what remains open; mitigation
  alone does not establish that an insight was unaffected. In interpretive work,
  account reflexively for how research relationships produce material rather than
  rewriting positionality as bias removal.

Use the [evidence index](corpus-index.md) for these version-specific analyses.
These roles can span several sentences or paragraphs. They are not sentence-count
targets, and they should not be imposed on an unrelated authorial argument. An
absent relation is not itself a defect; use it only when the argument depends on it.

## An actionable repair procedure

1. **Diagnose:** identify the missing relation, unsupported leap, redundant job,
   unclear referent or overloaded sentence. Give a short diagnosis only if useful
   to the researcher; do not replace the requested rewrite with a full rubric.
2. **Keep the information:** preserve evidence, condition names, citation scope,
   quantities and qualifiers. If evidence is missing, mark the gap explicitly.
3. **Reorder the reasoning:** put necessary definitions before their use; keep
   evidence with the claim it supports; group a contrast before its consequence.
4. **Connect entities:** make the next sentence's subject refer to an identifiable
   preceding idea. Replace ambiguous “this” with the relevant finding or distinction.
5. **Tune prose:** shorten noun stacks, use specific verbs, and remove metadiscourse
   that adds no orientation. Keep technical terms when they are the precise terms.
6. **Read across the boundary:** check the previous paragraph's ending and the next
   paragraph's beginning. A locally polished paragraph can still duplicate its neighbor.

## Original hypothetical example: group-level inference

Assumed supplied evidence for this illustration: an interview study contains reports
of greater willingness to speak; it contains no observed group-performance measure.

Before: “Our results highlight the transformative potential of the interface.
Participants felt more confident. This improves inclusive group decision-making.
Furthermore, AI can empower marginalized voices.”

After: “Participants described feeling more willing to challenge the group's
initial preference when they could prepare a response privately. Their accounts
suggest that private preparation reduced the perceived interpersonal cost of
speaking. The interviews do not establish whether more dissent entered the actual
discussion or changed the group's decision. Future evaluation could examine those
two outcomes separately.”

The rewrite links reported experience→interpretation→unmeasured outcome→next
question. It does not add numerical results or claim that the hypothetical
mechanism was tested. Actual drafting must use the manuscript's evidence, not
copy these assumed findings.

## Two-paragraph example: interpret an excerpt, then change perspective

**Entirely hypothetical training material, including the quotation. Never insert it
as participant evidence.** Assume a supplied packet contains a maker's account,
“I leave a note on the broken printer so the next person doesn't start over,” and
a coordinator's account that these notes are collected only during a weekly check.
It contains no observations of actual repair times.

Paragraph 1: “The maker described the printer note as a handoff to the next person:
‘I leave a note on the broken printer so the next person doesn't start over.’
The phrase ‘start over’ frames the note as preserving work already done, beyond
identifying a faulty machine. In this account, leaving the note transfers the next
step to an anticipated reader.”

Paragraph 2: “That anticipated reader does not necessarily encounter the note when
it is left. The coordinator described collecting notes during a weekly check.
Read together, the accounts locate a timing mismatch between leaving a handoff and
receiving it. They suggest an analytical question about how the coordination routine
makes responsibility visible between those moments.”

The first paragraph locates the speaker, provides the excerpt, extracts a specific
feature of its wording, and develops the interpretation. The second inherits
“anticipated reader,” changes perspective, introduces the contrasting account and
raises a relational question. The two accounts do not establish how long any repair
took. Keep that check in drafting reasoning unless the manuscript makes a timing
claim needing correction. A stronger institutional explanation would require further
material about the coordination routine; the last sentence makes that step a question.

This is one possible handoff, not a mandatory claim–quote–contrast formula. A rich
vignette may require sustained narration, and a verified quantitative result may
need a comparison rather than a quotation. Preserve real supplied excerpts exactly,
attribute them, and distinguish interpretation from paraphrase.

## Natural academic prose

Delete stock claims of novelty, importance or inevitability unless they do real
argumentative work. Prefer naming the finding to saying “these findings underscore
important implications.” Avoid repeatedly ending paragraphs with a broad design
promise. Vary paragraph length according to work performed, not to create cosmetic
rhythm. Preserve a small amount of purposeful repetition for construct continuity.
Do not blacklist every transition, passive sentence, first-person pronoun or
three-item list: each can be the right choice. The diagnostic question is whether
the prose makes the reasoning precise and readable, not whether it resembles a
stereotype of human or AI writing.
