# Conceptual, theoretical and critical arguments

Use when the central contribution is a concept, framework, distinction, explanation,
critique or research agenda. Literature, cases and historical materials can be the
argument's evidence. An empirical study is not compulsory, but the argument still
needs clear premises, accountable interpretation, substantive support and scope.

## Identify the intellectual operation

State what changes: a familiar concept is redefined; apparently similar practices
are distinguished; a hidden assumption is challenged; competing accounts are compared;
a framework connects previously separated observations; or a normative agenda is
proposed. Identify the reader consequence: what can now be explained, questioned,
designed or evaluated differently? A new label without such work is a weak claim.

Separate conceptual analysis from systematic literature synthesis. [P68, What Is
Interaction?](https://doi.org/10.1145/3025453.3025765) purposively compares theories
and explicitly does not claim exhaustive review. [P69, HCI Research as Problem-Solving](https://doi.org/10.1145/2858036.2858283)
uses a curated award-paper application to illustrate a philosophical proposal;
that sample does not establish field-wide prevalence or validate the philosophy.

## Build an argumentative spine

A useful starting sequence is consequential confusion/tension → concepts and
intellectual lineage → differentiated argumentative steps → worked cases or
counterexamples → implications and objections → bounded conclusion. Definitions
may need to be constructed through analysis rather than announced fully formed.
Organize headings so they reveal dependencies, not only broad topics.

Other valid structures include comparing several perspectives before introducing a
new distinction; historical cases followed by cross-case concepts; critique followed
by a constructive alternative; or an agenda grounded in neighboring fields. [P49,
Feminist HCI](https://doi.org/10.1145/1753326.1753521) introduces a tradition, examines
adjacent fields and HCI examples, then proposes qualities. [P53, Recollecting Craft](https://doi.org/10.1145/3610041)
uses historical cases before cross-case provocations. Neither is an incomplete
empirical paper waiting for a Methods section.

## Opening and literature as reasoning

An abstract should name the conceptual problem, the operation undertaken, the
central argument or distinctions and their consequence. An introduction should
make the tension understandable and explain why it matters to research or practice.
A gap can be an evaluative convention that misconstrues knowledge, as in [P48,
Implications for Design](https://doi.org/10.1145/1124772.1124855), rather than an
uncharted application area. Avoid overstating consensus merely to make the critique
appear more decisive.

Literature is often distributed through the argument. Distinguish sources used to
state an opposing position, provide a conceptual resource, establish history,
illustrate a case or support an empirical premise. Represent the strongest relevant
version of the position being criticized. Explain what your selection includes and
excludes; purposive selection can be appropriate but should not masquerade as
exhaustive coverage. Attribute inherited ideas and specify the transformation.

An integrative agenda may repeat local motivation → related work → research
proposal units, then reconnect them in a synthesis section. [P74, The Future of
Crowd Work](https://doi.org/10.1145/2441776.2441923) uses this architecture; worker
comments enrich and contest the agenda without validating its proposed future.

## Warrants and counterarguments

For each main claim, ask what kind of support it needs: conceptual coherence,
historical/documentary grounding, a comparative reading, empirical evidence from
existing studies, or an explicit normative commitment. Keep illustrative examples
separate from independent tests. An example can show how a distinction works
without proving its general usefulness.

Develop serious counterarguments where they sharpen scope. State the objection,
answer what can be answered, and retain any concession. P69's curiosity objection
ends by acknowledging what the proposed account does not explain. [P50, Research
Through Design](https://doi.org/10.1145/2207676.2208538) makes competing interpretations
of non-convergence visible instead of treating one as an observed cause. A
conceptual proposal can be valuable without eliminating every alternative.

A framework can yield competing readings of the same case. [P76, Reflective
Informatics](https://doi.org/10.1145/2702123.2702234) reads ChatterBox as both
requiring and potentially constraining transformation. Retain an unresolved contrast
when it exposes a conceptual boundary rather than forcing a decisive conclusion.

In discussion, explain how the concept changes a reading, design question or
research judgment. Distinguish “this framework draws attention to...” from “this
framework improves outcomes,” which would require a different warrant. A critical
paper may expose an unresolved tension rather than end with a feature list.

## Paragraph progression and original illustration

Useful patterns include **accepted position → overlooked assumption → counterexample
→ revised distinction**, **definition problem → intuitive case → formal distinction
→ consequence**, and **objection → reply → concession → bounded stance**. P68's
constructive-power paragraph reaches its definition after intermediate reasoning;
forcing the final definition into its first sentence would obscure its derivation.

**Hypothetical revision:** “We validate group experience as a new paradigm” becomes
“We distinguish the experience of taking part in a group process from an aggregate
of individual interface judgments. The distinction directs attention to shared
interpretations and unequal participation; the present argument does not establish
a validated measurement instrument.” This is an original illustration, not a claim
about the user's actual theory or evidence. Supply the manuscript's genuine
premises, comparisons and cases.

## Execute and review

Outline claims with their premises and support; draft the strongest central
comparison; develop definitions and objections; then revise the introduction to
promise that argument. Compress repeated framing before removing counterexamples
or conceptual distinctions. Preserve terminology consistently across `.tex` files.
Ask whether the proposal does intellectual work, whether sources are interpreted
fairly, whether scope is clear, and whether normative commitments are explicit
rather than presented as empirical facts.
