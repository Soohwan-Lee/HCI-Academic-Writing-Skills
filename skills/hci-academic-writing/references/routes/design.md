# Design knowledge and research through design

Use when making, experiencing, documenting and reflecting on designs produces the
paper's central knowledge. The contribution may be a concept, sensitizing quality,
annotated portfolio, design tension or account of practice. It is not automatically
a claim that the artifact outperforms alternatives. Identify what the designs help
readers understand and how the inquiry makes that understanding inspectable.

## Establish the design inquiry

Locate the concern, research stance, materials, situated encounters, design process,
reflections and proposed knowledge. Ask which choices or experiences made the
insight possible. Separate intended qualities from interpretations developed later.
Do not retroactively claim that every decision was planned to test the final concept.

There is disagreement about what research through design should produce and how
it should be assessed. [P13, Research Through Design as a Method](https://doi.org/10.1145/1240624.1240704)
and [P50, What Should We Expect From Research Through Design?](https://doi.org/10.1145/2207676.2208538)
should not be collapsed into one consensual checklist. State the manuscript's
commitments and explain the adequacy of its account rather than imposing universal
replicability, convergence or hypothesis-testing criteria. Concrete performance
claims still need evidence appropriate to those claims.

## Choose a spine that carries the knowledge

Possible organizations include concern/positioning → design process → artifacts
and encounters → reflection/annotations → contribution and boundaries; a portfolio
organized by cases followed by cross-case dimensions; or an unfolding narrative
where making and reflection alternate. Context, researcher positionality and material
specificity may be central evidence rather than preliminary background.

[P42, Photobox](https://doi.org/10.1145/2317956.2318055) connects material and temporal
design choices to an inquiry into slowness. [P43, Mechanical Sympathy](https://doi.org/10.1145/3643834.3661514)
articulates qualities retrospectively. [P46, Annotated Portfolio](https://doi.org/10.1145/3196709.3196745)
uses relations among research products to develop a conceptual account. Preserve
these differences instead of converting each into “design → user study → guidelines.”

## Write the opening and literature relation

An abstract can name the concern, describe the design inquiry and materials, state
the resulting insight or qualities, and explain their relevance. Avoid substituting
“we designed three prototypes” for the knowledge produced through them. Introduction
should make the concern consequential and the design approach appropriate; the gap
can be an impoverished framing, an unexplored experiential possibility or a tension
in existing practice, not only an absent feature.

Related work positions the inquiry among design traditions, concepts, materials and
prior artifacts. Explain what you inherit and what you reinterpret. Connect theory
to decisions or readings of the designs. If a concept emerges during reflection,
report that role instead of pretending it dictated the whole process. [P15, Crip
Reflections on Designing with Plants](https://doi.org/10.1145/3643834.3661509) is a
contrast to a detached universal user voice: situated embodied knowledge matters.

## Make process and interpretation inspectable

Select episodes because they change the inquiry, reveal a tension or make a concept
concrete. A chronological diary is not automatically an argument; explain what each
included episode contributes. Annotate images with the relevant material choice,
encounter or interpretation rather than only identifying the object. Show abandoned
possibilities when they explain why an insight emerged, not to exhaustively recount
all iterations.

Differentiate built, encountered, imagined and proposed elements. [P44, Morse Things](https://doi.org/10.1145/3064663.3064734)
uses expert co-speculation; that is not ordinary adoption evidence. [P45, Undertable](https://doi.org/10.1145/3643834.3660698)
requires attention to the context of experiencing a remake. [P47, Making Core
Memory](https://doi.org/10.1145/3173574.3174105) links making to historical interpretation;
missing historical voices remain consequential.

Discussion should expose relationships across artifacts or encounters, explain
what they sensitize designers to, and identify conditions of transfer. Keep
productive ambiguity when it is the subject of inquiry. A set of transferable
questions or tensions may be more faithful than prescriptive guidelines. Describe
how readers can work with the knowledge without claiming universal outcomes.

## Paragraph flow and original illustration

A reflective paragraph can progress **material encounter → felt or observed tension
→ design response → interpretation → remaining ambiguity**. A portfolio paragraph
can progress **shared quality → contrasting manifestations → relation between them
→ conceptual implication**. The main interpretation may legitimately come last.
When a topic-first claim would be opaque without the encounter, preserve the setup.

**Hypothetical revision:** “Our prototype proves that slow feedback increases
mindfulness” becomes “The delayed feedback became a resource for reflecting on
waiting during the documented encounters. We interpret that tension as a design
possibility for making anticipation perceptible; we did not measure an increase in
mindfulness.” Use actual fieldnotes, images and participant accounts to support the
interpretation, rather than treating this example as evidence.

## Execute and review

Outline the knowledge and pivotal episodes; draft annotated cases; develop relations
across them; reconcile abstract and contribution claims. Revise for explicit links
between material specificity and conceptual insight. Compress routine chronology
before removing the encounter that earns an interpretation. In `.tex`, preserve
image/caption relationships and accessible descriptions. Ask whether a reader can
see how the inquiry generated knowledge, whether the stance is coherent, and whether
an intended design quality has been mistaken for a demonstrated effect.

## Practice accounts and pictorial argument

[P128, Design Bookkeeping](https://doi.org/10.1145/3643834.3660754) (pictorial,
PDF pp.1–12) makes otherwise peripheral managerial work part of design knowledge.
Its six visual cases connect records, material encounters and interpretations.
Treat the visual arrangement as an argumentative sequence: identify which image
or entry establishes the situation, which detail changes the interpretation, and
how the caption or adjacent prose carries that change. A text-only section outline
cannot substitute for inspecting those relationships.

An opening may show the finished work before explaining the lens. Related work
can be distributed through cases when each concept's role remains recoverable.
Its p.4 paragraph moves from an external theoretical account to creative practice
and a guiding question; its p.7 account moves from embodied material inspection
to a workable local response and a modest transfer proposition. These are useful
alternatives to mandatory topic-first assertions. Ask whether the reader has enough
context to understand the claim, not whether every paragraph starts with one.

Distinguish a lens for noticing practice, a documentation genre and a procedure
others could follow. An invitation to adapt specialized records is not evidence
that novices can replicate the practice. Preserve first-person responsibility and
situated interpretation; extract transferable questions without erasing the
conditions that made the original insight possible.
