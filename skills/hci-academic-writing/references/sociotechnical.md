# Write sociotechnical understanding

Use when the contribution concerns how people, technologies, practices, norms and
institutions shape, sustain or constitute a phenomenon. This is an analytical object, not a synonym
for interviews, qualitative methods, CSCW or a socially important topic.

For local paragraph edits, read “Construct paragraphs” first; expand only when
attribution, analytical level or cultural interpretation changes. Use this guide
with qualitative, quantitative, documentary or mixed-source evidence as appropriate.

## Establish what the paper makes intelligible

Distinguish a situated characterization from an account of relationships and
processes. Identify which carries the main claim; a paper may do both. Both can contribute; neither must invent theory or build a system.
Specify the analytical level: interaction, individual, group, organization,
platform, institution or historical formation. If discussion changes level, show
what material, theoretical argument or prior scholarship warrants that move.
Participant accounts of pressure do not by themselves establish a national cause.

P110 interprets God-Saeng routines through tools, commitments and uncertainty;
P36 treats data integration as coordination; P33 examines labor reconfiguration;
P98 extends an interdependence account through accessibility negotiation. These cases show different
objects, not a universal sequence all sociotechnical papers must follow.

## Give each section an analytical job

An opening can establish a recognizable practice, unsettle an existing account,
identify what remains unintelligible, then explain the access and analytical
approach. State what the account changes for readers. Do not reduce its gap to
“few studies of population X” or invent a claim that all earlier work ignores society.

Related work can distinguish: context needed to understand the case; substantive
accounts of the phenomenon; theoretical lenses; and design/technical assumptions
being reconsidered. Choose headings by these dependencies. Explain how literatures
connect or disagree instead of presenting an author-by-author inventory. A source
about cultural background remains a cited background claim, not a study finding.

Findings may follow practices, configurations, trajectories, tensions or comparative
cases. Keep a participant's explanation, an observed episode and the analyst's
interpretation attributed. Illustrate variation and contrary cases. A quotation
is evidence to interpret, not a self-explanatory substitute for analysis.

Discussion should clarify the explanatory gain: what relationship was invisible,
what assumption now needs qualification, or what familiar concept reads differently.
Design implications can follow, but refusal, institutional change, conceptual
reframing or an unresolved tension may be the appropriate consequence. Do not force
an interface feature list or claim effectiveness for an untested recommendation.

## Construct paragraphs as inferential steps

A useful pattern is **analytical proposition → located material → interpretation
of the relationship → scope/transition**. An editorial alternative, not a measured pattern here, begins with an episode and earns
the interpretive proposition afterward. Topic-first structure is useful when
the reader can already understand the claim; preserve an unfolding account when
that is how the evidence becomes intelligible.

At sentence level, retain the analytical object while changing what is known about
it. One possible move is from actors' action to its technical/social arrangement;
a consequence or tension needs its own evidentiary support. Pronouns must identify a recoverable actor or arrangement. Words such
as “therefore” and “thus” cannot supply a missing warrant. A new abstract noun should
name a relationship already shown, rather than replace the evidence with jargon.

P110 arXiv v1 §4.1.2 opens by moving from participants' assembly of arrangements, to their
social/technical composition, to patterns elaborated next. Its §5.1 develops an
interpretive construct by explaining obligations and accountability. These are
source-specific examples, not three-sentence rules or wording to transplant.
Check the bridge back to findings; naming is not theory generation. P36's discussion
p.20 opens with a concession, locates hidden prior coordination, then returns to
the studied researchers. P33 p.19 qualifies an inclusion aspiration, develops its
tensions through evidence and ends with an unresolved question. These contrasting
moves prevent a single topic-first recipe from displacing the argument.

At claim-bearing sentences, level changes and weak transitions ask: what does it inherit, what does it add, and
what permits that addition? If the transition goes from an interview account to
an institutional explanation, retain attribution and supply the bridge or narrow
the claim. Keep tension when the same arrangement affords support and pressure.

## Preserve access, culture and uncertainty

Explain which recruitment, relationships, positionality, translation and elicitation
choices materially shape the claim or material. Never infer missing details; narrow
the claim or mark a concise author-supply comment when needed. Name whose perspective
the material contains: participants' accounts of absent parties are not those parties'
own testimony. Build power claims through contrasts in position, consequences and
institutional constraints, not one interpersonal episode. Probes can intervene while producing accounts; do not write
elicited reflections as unobtrusively observed routines. Name the limits of a short
study rather than adding a conventional longitudinal trajectory.

A culturally specific term can be an emic category, analytical device or public
discourse; specify which use the materials support. Explain translation choices
and avoid treating a nation or community as internally uniform. Neither exoticize
the case nor erase the context to manufacture universality.

For computational or documentary accounts, check provenance, collection boundaries
and the relation between traces/documents and practices. Counts do not alone reveal
meaning, and an archive's silence does not establish that a practice never existed.
Use the matching method guide for the evidence actually available.
