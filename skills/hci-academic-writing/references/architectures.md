# Choose and develop a manuscript architecture

Use for a whole-paper outline, a substantial section draft, or structural revision.
The unit of planning is **section → subsection → paragraph → sentence relation**.
Do not substitute an Introduction/Methods/Results list for an argument plan. Read
the relevant contribution route as well; these alternatives are conditional aids,
not venue templates or an exhaustive taxonomy.

## Choose the spine from dependencies

Ask what must become intelligible first: a practice/problem, a new capability,
a material encounter, an intervention contrast, or a conceptual dispute. Identify
the source of design commitments and the knowledge object the manuscript delivers.
A formative study is one possible origin, not a required ceremony. Distinguish
literature-derived goals, technical constraints, participant accounts, negotiated
commitments, prior practice and retrospective interpretation.

The same venue can publish several architectures. A paper containing a system,
interviews and an evaluation can still be primarily empirical, conceptual or design
research. Find the contribution's argument before arranging the activities.

## Source-grounded structural alternatives

### Capability first, rationale second

[P125 Wheeler](https://doi.org/10.1145/3654777.3676396), author PDF §§3–6,
introduces interaction modes, explains ideation/refinement, then describes layered
implementation and capability-specific evaluations. Its initial mapping draws on
prior work; participatory sessions refine embodiments. It has no obligatory
standalone Design Goals section.

A working outline can develop:

- **Overview:** user-visible modes → worked action → boundary case → mode switching.
- **Rationale:** problem/action cost → inherited mechanism and difference → physical
  constraint and rejected alternative → participatory embodiment choices → iterations.
- **Implementation:** components in their explanatory dependency order.
- **Evaluation:** shared setup once → capability-specific tasks/procedures → measured
  outcomes → experience/failure analysis → distinct situated-use evidence if available.

Within rationale, a paragraph can compare alternatives, locate an unresolved bodily
constraint, introduce a combined design and explain why another option conflicts
with the interaction. It should deliver a decision, not a chronological list of
prototypes. Use this spine when readers need to see the capability before its
rationale makes sense.

### Formative inquiry first, then design translation

[P134 Chart4Blind](https://doi.org/10.1145/3640543.3645175), arXiv v2 §§3–6,
contains a nested formative method/findings sequence, design principles, system
modules/interface, creator usability study and recipient output exploration.

A working outline can develop:

- **Formative inquiry:** scope/participants → actual elicitation and analysis →
  findings grouped by consequential work → design translation.
- **Design commitments:** trace each goal to its source; distinguish a finding from
  the authors' chosen response. Do not invent an interview to justify a known goal.
- **System:** end-to-end walkthrough → modules → interactions that enact goals.
- **Evaluation:** separate perspectives or claims where they need different tasks
  and evidence; do not merge creators and recipients into an unspecified “user.”

A finding subsection can use paragraph 1 to compare existing workflows and locate
burden; paragraph 2 to identify common steps, obstacles and the chosen requirement.
A following subsection can narrow to costly operations, then another to output needs.
This is one useful scale progression, not a required three-subsection structure.

### Reflective cases and visual argument

[P128 Design Bookkeeping](https://doi.org/10.1145/3643834.3660754), PDF pp.2–12,
uses finished work/context → reflective lens → six visual cases → cross-case
interpretation → possible cultivation/circulation. Each case identifies material,
explains emergence and interprets what it makes intelligible.

A working case page can contain a knowledge-oriented title, an object descriptor,
an origin paragraph, an interpretive paragraph and a connected visual sequence.
The origin paragraph explains the material problem and response. The interpretation
paragraph explains what the encounter changes in understanding. Caption/image
relationships can carry a transformation that prose alone does not show.

Cross-case discussion should change scale: relation among cases → dimensions of
that relation → audience/transfer conditions → possible use. Do not replace this
with a generic effectiveness evaluation. Other RtD forms need other spines; a
longitudinal deployment or speculative encounter may turn on time and relationships
rather than a repeated case-page format.

### Intervention comparison with explanatory follow-up

[P103 Devil's Advocate](https://doi.org/10.1145/3640543.3645199), PDF §§3–5,
places RQs inside Study Design, followed by task, treatments, procedure and measures.
Results move through comparison questions and then exploratory conversation analysis.
Discussion develops outcome puzzles and implications.

A working outline can develop:

- **Study design:** questions → task → manipulated conditions → procedure → measures
  and units. Explain implemented conditions where needed to understand the contrast.
- **Results:** primary contrast → factors/conditional contrasts → different outcome
  perspectives → exploratory material, if that is the actual design.
- **Discussion:** central answer and possible explanation → boundary/puzzle →
  competing interpretations → design/research consequence.

For group research, group decisions, individual perceptions and conversation traces
may meet in a discussion paragraph but remain distinct evidence. A six-sentence
puzzle paragraph can establish contrasting outcomes, propose an explanation, explain
its relevance, derive a tension and motivate inquiry. Do not copy the sentence
count; preserve the dependency that makes the interpretation understandable.

### Situated relations and conceptual extension

[P129 Porous by Design](https://doi.org/10.1145/3643834.3661552), author PDF
§§4–5, organizes findings by personhood, safety and isolation; discussion asks about
porosity and worker-centered design. Interview material has an independent social
and conceptual contribution, not merely a role in requirements gathering.

A working discussion can develop paragraph 1 around the observed relation and its
empirical referents; paragraph 2 around conceptual lineage, difference and a concrete
example; paragraph 3 around its implications for responsibility. Another subsection
may proceed benefit → unequal costs/complication → qualified direction. Keep the
complication next to the direction it changes, rather than bury it in generic limits.

A normative or conceptual endpoint does not require a future prototype to become a
contribution. Use the qualitative and sociotechnical guides for other forms; this
example is not a universal theme-count or interview-paper structure.

### Literature map, then conceptual synthesis

[P123 Digital Legacy](https://doi.org/10.1145/3610059), author PDF pp.7–23,
separates thematic findings from a lifecycle interpretation, then develops
interdependencies, multiple actors, generations and an agenda. It does not repeat
the analyzed corpus in a standalone Related Work section.

A synthesis subsection can move through observed relationships → conceptual
analogy/ancestry → examples across papers → departure from the antecedent model
→ consolidated model and intended use. A subsequent subsection should exercise
the model: identify an overly simple reading, supply different relational cases,
consider an awkward boundary case, then motivate a question. Do not confuse the
number of coded themes with the dimensions or stages of the conceptual output.
Other review forms need other spines; use the review route to distinguish mapping,
critical interpretation and effect synthesis.

### Instrument construction, application, then a secondary artifact

[P121 Research Ethics/Openness/Transparency](https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3580848)
places criteria construction before the audit method/results, then adds a screening
prototype with its own rationale, implementation and evaluation. The prototype did
not generate the manually coded audit findings.

For a method contribution, a construction subsection can explain origins/refinement,
version/applicability, then rationale and reuse. An application subsection answers a
different question: how the instrument was applied to a sample. If automation is a
secondary branch, distinguish the envisioned human workflow from the implemented
component before reporting its evidence. Design considerations can be derived from
heterogeneous tasks and error costs without a fabricated formative study.

### Critical questions, reframing and practice

[P111 Non-design](https://doi.org/10.1145/1978942.1979275), a four-page author
manuscript, progresses through questions about alternatives, harms and computational
problem transformations. Discussion interprets this progression and challenges the
problem–solution premise; the ending gives implications for research practice.

A critical-case subsection can start with an evaluative question and familiar
contrast, develop one case, then use another to change the analytical scale and
prepare the next question. Discussion may need positioning → distinction →
concession/counterargument → reframing rather than a results recap. Keep the
premises and case reasoning inspectable. Do not invent a Methods section, empirical
validation or a final prototype when the contribution is a defensible position.

### Intervention, infrastructure and maintenance

[P112 Turkopticon](https://doi.org/10.1145/2470654.2470742) develops stance and
institutional context before motivation and system arrangements; discussion turns
to maintenance, strategic quantification, publics and ambivalent success.

An infrastructure subsection can explain the coordination problem, the initial
arrangement, what participation exposed, and the adjustment or unresolved cost.
A maintenance subsection can move from concrete support work to dependencies and
then to what sustaining the intervention means. Keep technical choices connected
to who must do the work. A generic usability study cannot replace this argument;
participation alone also does not establish community control.

### Resource construction and differentiated utility

P132 UICrit (UIST 2024; source record in the corpus) separates collection, dataset
characterization and applications with nested task-specific methods/results.

A construction closing paragraph can deliver effort → resulting records → schema
→ examples → analysis handoff. Characterization subsections can use analytical
procedure → categories/distribution → comparison to an external space → coverage
interpretation. Utility branches then explain the task, configuration and evidence.
Do not collapse resource coverage, task performance and prospective reuse into one
claim. For benchmarks or repositories, adapt the utility branch to their actual use.

### Historical sequence followed by cross-period interpretation

P115's sociotechnical genealogy (CSCW 2026; source record in the corpus) separates
four eras from three cross-era analytical dimensions. Historical sections establish
changes that later interpretive subsections can connect.

A dimension subsection can begin with an inherited framing, examine a documented
case, state the interpretive shift, and develop its temporal, relational or
institutional implications. Readers need the case before its conceptual consequence.
A chronological narrative alone does not perform the cross-period synthesis;
repeating each era in discussion also leaves the interpretive work undone. Keep
historical actors' accounts, documentary evidence and the authors' judgments legible.

### Concept development through an illustrative design

[P119 Social Transparency](https://doi.org/10.1145/3411764.3445188), arXiv v1
§§2–6, develops conceptual lineage, scenario-based design exploration, interview
methods, interpreted contexts and theoretical discussion.

The exploration section can progress through concept scope → why this material
supports inquiry → how its elements emerged → relationship to prior concepts →
audience/scenario choice → consequential encounter details. The findings develop
what the concept helps explain and where it struggles. Unlike a formative-first
system paper, the illustrative design supports conceptual development; interviews
need not become either requirements or an effectiveness test.

## Produce an actionable outline

For a substantial outline request, provide enough detail to draft from:

1. **Section purpose and position:** what the reader learns here and why here.
2. **Subsection sequence:** the questions each answers; merge duplicated jobs.
3. **Paragraph plan:** one working claim/question and its supporting material per
   paragraph; name the logical handoff, not just “transition.”
4. **One representative sentence flow:** role sequence and referents for a difficult
   paragraph. Expand to full prose when the user asks for drafting.

Use author-supplied evidence and actual project files. Keep these planning labels in
working notes or requested outlines; do not insert them into the finished manuscript.
For a small revision, repair the relevant dependency directly without imposing a
full outline or new project artifact.

Before choosing a structure, ask what would break if two sections or paragraphs
swapped. If nothing changes and their jobs overlap, consider consolidation. If a
later claim depends on an earlier definition, case or comparison, preserve that
order or provide the necessary preview. An early system overview and a later design
rationale can both be legitimate even when research occurred in another order.

Coverage note: these eight source-specific contrasts are an initial architecture
layer. They do not yet cover every contribution family. For resources, historical, participatory and other inquiries, and for alternatives
within the families above, also use their existing route/catalogue guidance and
select suitable source examples instead of forcing one of these eight forms. No optimal paragraph counts or venue-wide frequencies
have been established.
