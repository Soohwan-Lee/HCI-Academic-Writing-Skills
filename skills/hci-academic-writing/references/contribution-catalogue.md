# Select the knowledge contribution

Use this guide for framing, outlines and edits that change claim scope. Skip it
for grammar-only or local flow requests. The categories are composable editorial
choices, not an exhaustive ontology or a ranking of research traditions.

## Find the relevant guidance

1. Find the sentence or passage carrying the claim.
2. Choose a family below: understand, reconsider, build, change with partners,
   or equip/synthesize.
3. Pick the closest row; if none fits, describe the claim directly.
4. Load the guide matching the warrant. Empirical theory use or critical analysis
   still needs its empirical evidence guide; the conceptual guide is an optional
   supplement, not a replacement for empirical reporting.
5. Start with one guide and add only the support needed for distinct obligations.

Read an exemplar's analysis before citing it. Row membership is not a frequency
estimate, and a partial illustration is not evidence of complete subtype coverage.

## Identify the claim before choosing the method guide

Ask what a reader can now understand, make, do or reconsider, and which passage
someone would cite for that change. Inspect the strongest supported claim, including
one hidden in the discussion. A topic (HCAI), venue (CSCW), method (interview), or
form (pictorial) does not answer this question.

When changing contribution statements, title/abstract claim scope, paper structure
or analytical level, make a compact working contract:

`claim operation → object and analytical level → reader use → warrant → boundary`

Add the role of theory when relevant: no explicit theoretical claim, sensitizing, application, extension,
revision or generation. Record secondary claims separately as tested/shown by the study,
interpreted from its material, or prospective. Do not burden a paragraph edit with a new intake form.
If two readings remain plausible, show the alternatives and the evidence needed
to choose; do not silently manufacture the stronger contribution.

The table below describes finer claim types and their writing consequences.
P-IDs are illustrations in the [corpus index](corpus-index.md), not proof that the
category is fully covered. Rows without a P-ID are editorial guidance awaiting
stronger direct exemplars in the expansion. Do not portray every row as a measured
pattern across the corpus.

## Understand practices, relationships and effects

| Claim type | Required distinction and writing consequence | Support |
|---|---|---|
| Situated characterization | Establish access, variation and why the account changes understanding. A population/context being new is not the entire argument. | P35, P38, P110; qualitative |
| Sociotechnical configuration or process | Explain relations among actors, technologies, norms and institutions rather than listing social and technical factors. Bridge analytical levels explicitly. | P33, P36, P98, P110; sociotechnical guide |
| Comparative or causal effect | Match contrasts and causal wording to the actual design, unit, uncertainty and alternatives. Organize results around the supported comparisons. | P03, P54; quantitative |
| Explanatory or predictive model | Distinguish prediction, statistical fit and explanation. Show how constructs and competing accounts relate to evidence. | P55; quantitative |
| Descriptive computational observation | Specify sampling and trace-to-construct mapping, missingness and measurement error. Large data do not ensure population coverage. | P64; quantitative |
| Algorithm/platform audit | State the criterion, sampling/probing strategy, time and affected groups; separate observed disparities from their causal explanation. | quantitative + method; no indexed direct exemplar |

For interpretive accounts load [qualitative](routes/empirical-qualitative.md), and
for statistical claims load [quantitative](routes/empirical-quantitative.md).
[Sociotechnical guidance](sociotechnical.md) supplements either when relationships
and configurations are the actual object of understanding.

## Develop concepts and reconsider assumptions

| Claim type | Required distinction and writing consequence | Support |
|---|---|---|
| Definition or conceptual distinction | Show the consequential confusion, boundary cases and what the distinction enables. A renamed theme alone is insufficient. | P68 |
| Sensitizing/intermediate concept | Show how it helps interpret or design across cases; describe context so readers can judge transfer, without promising a general law. | P43, P46 |
| Framework, typology or design space | Explain derivation, dimensions, relationships, use and boundary cases; distinguish illustrative coverage from exhaustiveness. | P76 |
| Theory application | Explain why this lens makes something newly intelligible. Application can support an empirical contribution without being theory invention. | P36; P110 illustrates sensitizing use |
| Theory generation | Connect constructs and relations to evidence/argument, scope and alternative accounts. Do not add causal predictions foreign to the research tradition. | conceptual + appropriate evidence route; no indexed direct exemplar |
| Theory revision/extension | State what the inherited account explains, where it is insufficient, what changes, and what remains. A new context alone is not revision. | P98; conceptual + qualitative |
| Critique of knowledge/evaluation | Engage the strongest relevant existing practice; expose a consequential assumption through evidence, counterexample or argument. | P04, P48, P69 |
| Structural or critical analysis | Make analytical commitments visible; claim a standpoint epistemology only when actually adopted; connect institutions, histories and unequal relations to the case. | P49, P15; P33 illustrates critical stance, not standpoint-derived evidence; P98 adds power analysis |
| Normative argument or agenda | Distinguish empirical and normative reasoning, recognizing that description may already be value-laden; state values, reasons, objections, tradeoffs and who bears costs. | P49, P74 |
| Non-design/refusal | Argue why an intervention is inappropriate, for whom and under what conditions; consider alternatives and decision authority. No feature list is required. | conceptual; no indexed direct exemplar |
| Historical account | Describe source provenance, selection, interpretation, periodization and silences. Do not infer absence of practice from absent records. | P09, P53 provide contrasts, not complete methodological coverage |
| Genealogy | Explain how a present assumption became possible through contingent changes and power relations. Chronological related work is not sufficient. | conceptual; no indexed direct exemplar |

Load the guide matching the warrant and use [conceptual](routes/conceptual.md)
for additional argument support. Empirically grounded theory and critique still
require empirical reporting; the heading above does not determine the method route. Historical and
normative papers need not imitate participant-study IMRaD or end in design features.

## Build capabilities and develop design knowledge

| Claim type | Required distinction and writing consequence | Support |
|---|---|---|
| Interaction technique | Explain the mechanism, operating conditions, comparisons and failure modes. A study stimulus is not automatically a technical contribution. | P56, P79 |
| System/application | Show the enabling capability through coherent tasks and architecture, then evaluate the claims made. | P01, P29 |
| Toolkit/programming abstraction | Specify expressiveness, supported users/tasks and limits; demonstrate breadth and evaluate the particular toolkit claims. | P12 supplies evaluation guidance |
| Algorithm/optimization | State objectives, constraints, baselines and technical limits; do not equate computational improvement with human benefit. | P24 |
| Hardware/material/fabrication | Make process, reproducibility, physical properties and limits inspectable; preserve diagrams as evidence. | P19, P22, P80, P81 |
| Platform/infrastructure capability | Explain representations, integration, downstream use and maintenance conditions. Separate technical infrastructure from social infrastructuring. | P27, P83 |
| RtD/material/portfolio knowledge | Trace making, encounters and reflection into transferable understanding; articulate relations across designs rather than listing artifacts. | P39, P42, P46 |
| Speculative/provocative design | Explain what the artifact makes discussable and how encounters support interpretation. Do not substitute an invented usability/effectiveness test. | P44, P45 |

Load [artifact](routes/artifact.md) or [design](routes/design.md). An artifact can
serve as an instrument, provocation or research outcome; identify its actual job.

## Change practices with partners

| Claim type | Required distinction and writing consequence | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Participatory design knowledge | Workshops alone do not establish shared power. Show who decided what, whose priorities changed the design and whose disagreement remains. | Decision traces, roles, consequential design changes |
| Action research | Describe collaborative problem formulation, cycles of action/reflection and grounds for claims of change. A deployment alone is insufficient. | Cycles, interpretation with partners, situated outcomes |
| Infrastructuring | Follow relationships, institutions and maintenance over time; do not imply a project became sustainable merely because it launched. | Maintenance work, dependencies, withdrawal and continuing conditions |
| Community/activist partnership | Explain reciprocity, ownership, risks and how partners judge outcomes; avoid automatic empowerment claims. | Partner perspectives, tensions and documented consequences |

P67 is a review of community-collaborative approaches, not direct evidence that
all these practices were performed in a particular study. Use it to locate the
literature; seek direct exemplars for concrete architectural decisions. Load
qualitative or design guidance as needed. Participation can also be a method within
a different contribution; do not require a separate change claim in every such paper.

A **probe is not inherently a contribution category**. It may elicit accounts,
inspire design, test technical feasibility or support practitioner inquiry. It can
intervene in everyday life without establishing a causal effectiveness claim. Name
what was introduced, what it changed in access to data, burdens and interpretation;
route by the resulting knowledge claim. P06 and P110 illustrate different uses.

## Equip, check and synthesize research

| Claim type | Required distinction and writing consequence | Support |
|---|---|---|
| New method/procedure | Explain the change to practice, assumptions, worked use and failure conditions; merely using a method is not a method contribution. | P58, P72, P77; method |
| Measurement instrument | Define construct, item development, scoring and evidence for reliability/validity in specified contexts. Translation does not establish invariance. | method; no indexed direct exemplar |
| Guidelines/evaluation framework | Separate derivation, specification and testing; make applicability and unsupported recommendations explicit. | P08, P12; method |
| Dataset/benchmark/resource | Show provenance, construction, units, coverage, access and stewardship. A released file alone is not its knowledge value. | P14, P28, P107–109; resource |
| Direct/close replication | Explain relationship to the original claim, deviations, sensitivity and comparable estimates; preserve the replication objective. | quantitative; no indexed direct exemplar |
| Conceptual replication/boundary test | Justify changed conditions; distinguish a boundary result from proof the original claim was false. | quantitative; no indexed direct exemplar |
| Null/equivalence evidence | Distinguish non-significance from evidence of absence; use the actual intervals, sensitivity or equivalence analysis, never invented tests. | quantitative; no indexed direct exemplar |
| Metascience/methodological critique | Explain sampling, coding and consequences for inference; avoid replacing systematic evidence with judgments about individual authors. | P04, P62 provide contrasting scopes |
| Scoping/mapping review | Make scope, selection and classification inspectable; explain what the map reveals beyond its categories. | P61, P65; review |
| Systematic review/meta-analysis | Match the question to eligibility, appraisal and synthesis; pooling requires comparability and treatment of heterogeneity. | P62 illustrates review, not proof of meta-analysis coverage |
| Critical/interpretive/theory-building synthesis | Explain selection and interpretive commitments, then develop the argument across sources. A long related-work section is not enough. | P51, P52, P67 offer contrasts; review |

Load [method](routes/method.md), [resource](routes/resource.md) or
[review](routes/review.md). A document analysis studies documents as empirical
objects (P16); it need not be a review synthesizing research findings.

For replication reporting, state the prespecified success criterion when available.
A failed replication under that criterion is not proof that the original effect is
absent everywhere. Report p-values and intervals without inventing alpha thresholds.
A translated instrument needs evidence appropriate to the measurement claim; do not
mandate a particular invariance test for every translation or conflate reliability,
validity and cross-group comparability.

## Combine without multiplying claims

Use [hybrids](routes/hybrids.md) when claims require genuinely different warrants.
Name the claim that organizes the paper; a second major claim needs its own warrant. Proposed implications
are not evaluated capabilities; an empirical account does not become a theory
contribution just because it uses theory. Choose evidence guides selectively rather
than loading every module associated with a word in the manuscript.
