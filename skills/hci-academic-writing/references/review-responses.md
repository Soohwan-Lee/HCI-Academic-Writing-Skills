# Rebuttal and reviewer-driven revision

Read the decision, meta-review and relevant comments alongside the actual manuscript.
Confirm whether the venue permits only a response, a revised manuscript, or both;
check the supplied length and timing constraints. A response is a reasoned account
of the research and changes, not an opportunity to invent evidence.

Make a compact working map: comment → underlying concern → response → evidence or
change → location → unresolved dependency. Group duplicate concerns while retaining
reviewer identifiers. Prioritize issues central to the decision and contribution,
then correctness, clarity and presentation. A meta-review helps interpret priorities
but does not excuse ignoring a serious methodological concern from another reviewer.

For each response, briefly quote or accurately paraphrase the concern, answer it
directly, explain supporting evidence or the revision, and give the section/location.
Use stable section names if line numbers will change. Produce or cite `latexdiff`
only when the project uses it and actual changes have been made. Keep acknowledgments
brief and specific; avoid repeated gratitude paragraphs before every answer.

Distinguish four kinds of action:

- **Clarification:** make an existing method, result or boundary visible and identify
  where it already existed or has now been clarified.
- **Reanalysis/additional evidence:** use actual available data and an authorized,
  appropriate analysis; describe what changed and what it establishes. Do not
  promise work the authors have not agreed or are unable to perform.
- **Claim adjustment:** narrow a statement when evidence cannot support the broader
  inference. Update abstract, contribution list and discussion consistently.
- **Reasoned disagreement:** explain why the requested criterion does not match the
  contribution or evidence, acknowledge any useful underlying concern, and improve
  the manuscript's explanation where warranted. Do not concede an unsupported
  interpretation merely to sound agreeable.

A request for a new experiment cannot always be solved through prose. Complete
supported edits, identify the remaining evidence decision, and avoid claiming the
new study was performed. A request that every ethnography produce product features
may instead call for clarifying its knowledge contribution and intended use.
For a mixed result, acknowledge the uncertainty rather than selecting only favorable
outcomes for the response.

**Hypothetical response structure:** “R2 asks whether the reported preference implies
better task performance. We agree that these outcomes differ. We have narrowed the
abstract and Discussion §5.2 to reported preference and added the absence of a
performance comparison to the claim boundary. The present data do not establish a
performance advantage.” Only say changes were made after making them.

Before delivery, check response–manuscript consistency, exact result values, added
citation support, unresolved promises and the actual response word count. Preserve
anonymization and the venue's permitted response content. A polished rebuttal should
make the evidence easier to assess, not promise acceptance.
