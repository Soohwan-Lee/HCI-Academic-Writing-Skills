# Contribution-dependent manuscript architecture

Status: active, 2026-10-31. The user superseded the 208-paper count target. This work is
completed through adequate structural coverage and usable guidance, not more titles. User examples of UIST systems and DIS RtD
illustrate the required depth, not the limits of coverage.

## Deliverables and evidence levels

Analyze four nested levels together: section sequence → subsection sequence →
paragraph jobs and transitions → sentence roles and referent chains. Keep three
outputs distinct:

- **Observed map:** actual headings and passage order in a named source version,
  with page/section locators. Paraphrase rhetorical jobs; do not reproduce prose.
- **Cross-paper comparison:** structural alternatives, what motivates them, and
  counterexamples. Two cases suggest an alternative, not a population norm.
- **Drafting guidance:** conditional choices an agent can apply to a manuscript.
  Mark editorial proposals as proposals, not observed source structures.

A paper-level note with two micro-analyses is not a full hierarchical map. The
existing 128 notes are a source base, not 128 completed four-level maps. Existing
numeric measurement remains seven body/section papers and 32 paragraphs in 14
papers; do not imply those samples grew when adding rhetorical maps.

## Coverage matrix

Use knowledge contribution, empirical/argumentative warrant and manuscript form
as separate axes. Compare within and across venues. At least two contrasting source
maps per family are a first synthesis gate, not an adequate final sample by itself.

| Family | Variants to compare | Structural question |
|---|---|---|
| Artifact/capability | formative-led, technique-led, interaction-led, infrastructure, toolkit, fabrication | Where do goals come from, and what must readers understand before implementation? |
| Empirical explanation/comparison | experiments, field observation, logs, longitudinal, replication/null | Are subsections organized by RQ, construct, contrast, study or temporal process? |
| Situated/sociotechnical account | practices, configurations, institutional power, culture, relational interpretation | How do episodes and voices become themes, relations or explanations? |
| Design/RtD | portfolio, reflective practice, material inquiry, soma/embodied, speculative, pictorial | How do making and interpretation alternate; what is the visual evidence's role? |
| Participatory/action work | co-design, partnership, community-led inquiry, infrastructuring, maintenance | How do commitments, negotiations, iterations and change become the contribution? |
| Conceptual/theoretical | extension, sensitizing concept, typology, framework, proposition, theory critique | How is the concept earned, differentiated and used? |
| Critical/normative/historical | position, genealogy, archival history, non-design, speculative critique | How are premises, historical periods, cases and counterarguments ordered? |
| Method/measurement | instrument, operationalization, protocol, evaluation method, metaresearch | How are construction, application and validation separated or interleaved? |
| Resource/benchmark | dataset, design repository, taxonomy resource, benchmark | How do provenance, representation, quality and utility support reuse? |
| Literature synthesis | systematic/scoping, interpretive, critical, methodological, meta-analysis | How do selection, mapping and synthesis lead to knowledge rather than a catalogue? |
| Hybrids/multiple studies | sequential exploration/intervention, independent complementary studies, mixed methods | Which dependencies justify the order and where does integration occur? |

Do not impose a contribution route solely because a method or a particular heading
appears. A formative study may motivate a system, be an independent situated account,
or be absent. Design goals may come from prior work, technical constraints, practice,
participatory decisions or explicit design commitments. Report their actual origin.

## Per-source extraction record

1. Identity, analyzed version, main-text boundaries, visual inspection and access limits.
2. Primary and secondary knowledge objects; role of each study/artifact/theory.
3. Actual section and subsection headings in order; each heading's reader question,
   output and dependency on earlier/later material. Preserve non-IMRaD organizations.
4. For selected consequential subsections, map **every paragraph**: locator, job,
   claim/question, material used, relation to predecessor, handoff to successor.
   Choose more than introduction: include construction/making/findings/synthesis.
5. For at least two mapped paragraphs, annotate every sentence: role, given/new
   information, referent carried forward, inferential turn and closure/handoff.
   Identify when topic-first, delayed interpretation or narrative opening works.
6. Explain what would break if sections/paragraphs were reordered. Identify valid
   alternative orders and the information they would need earlier.
7. Separate observed structures from proposed repairs. Record an anti-pattern only
   when it follows from the passage, not from an assumption about AI writing.
8. Add comparable length measurements only with explicit extraction rules and
   denominators; exclude captions/references or report them separately.
